# When does interaction identify a hidden embodiment?

**A robot can infer a control-relevant property of its body from practice. Our Panda–Sawyer reader has yet to show that it does.** A commanded movement and the measured response can reveal an action-frame rotation, a delay, or a joint limit. A model can also learn to recognize a familiar arm, predict the next movement, or pick a useful correction. Those are distinct achievements with different tests. The strongest precedents change a known property within one robot, compare the estimate or response against ground truth, and test the resulting controller. [UP-OSI, §IV](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss13/p48.pdf) · [Rapid Embodiment Adaptation, §§III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01506v2)

## What the learner is trained to infer

| Approach | Information available and route into control | What was demonstrated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Estimate named physical variables.** Yu et al., *Preparing for the Unknown* (RSS 2017). | A universal policy trains with simulator parameters. A supervised online system identifier learns to estimate those same parameters from recent state–action history. The policy accepts the estimate where it used to receive ground truth. | The double-pendulum test varied a signed center-of-mass displacement and compared true and estimated values. This is a direct identification check. Its supervision and control interface specify what the coordinates mean. [Algorithms 1–2 and §IV](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss13/p48.pdf) |
| **Predict a control-relevant teacher embedding.** Kumar et al., *Rapid Motor Adaptation* (RSS 2021), and Li et al., *Rapid Embodiment Adaptation for Quadrupedal Locomotion* (2026 preprint). | RMA first trains the policy and a privileged dynamics encoder. A history encoder then predicts the teacher embedding, allowing the base policy to run on proprioceptive history. Li et al. explicitly compare regressing physical quantities versus a latent that goes directly to a frozen action decoder. Their model retains fixed structural descriptors. | RMA adapted a quadruped to terrain and payload changes. Li et al. tested joint limits and trunk mass on a Go2, comparing with nominal conditioning and an oracle. This is a close precedent for a frozen motor receiving inferred context. Both motors were originally trained to consume the relevant conditioning. A learned embedding can merge physically different settings that call for the same action. [RMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034) · [Li et al., §§III-B–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01506v2) |
| **Learn latent dynamics from prediction.** Doshi-Velez & Konidaris, *Hidden Parameter Markov Decision Processes* (IJCAI 2016). | Transitions grouped by instance train a shared dynamics model. New interactions update a posterior over instance-specific basis weights. Prediction grounds the weights without named physical labels. | Eight Acrobot training mass pairs and a 16-pair evaluation grid (including familiar pairs) demonstrate parameter sharing. In Cartpole, better dynamics prediction coexisted with a common successful balancing strategy. A useful prediction representation can therefore have little value for choosing an action. [§§3–5, Tables 1–2](https://cs.brown.edu/people/gdk/pubs/hip_mdps.pdf) |
| **Infer a task posterior.** Rakelly et al., *PEARL* (ICML 2019), and Zintgraf et al., *VariBAD* (ICLR 2020). | PEARL combines transition evidence into a distribution and samples a latent for the policy. Critic loss supplies task-relevant supervision. VariBAD uses a recurrent encoder and a decoder trained to predict task observations such as rewards, then conditions its policy on a representation of the belief. | PEARL's randomized Walker used 40 train and 10 test dynamics settings. VariBAD illustrates how beliefs separate opposite HalfCheetah reward directions. Return and posterior plots demonstrate adaptation to a task distribution. A hidden reward, a dynamics change, and a physical arm have different meanings even when the algorithm calls each one a task. [PEARL, paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rakelly19a/rakelly19a.pdf) · [PEARL, Appendix A](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rakelly19a/rakelly19a-supp.pdf) · [VariBAD, §5 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08348) |
| **Choose interactions that reveal the variable.** Zhou, Pinto & Gupta, *Environment Probing Interaction Policies* (ICLR 2019). | A ten-step probe is rewarded for producing a context that improves prediction of independent transitions. The downstream task policy is trained with this context. | In simulated Striker, 25 training combinations varied object mass and damping. Mean miss distance on held-out settings was 0.162 m after the learned probe, 0.259 m with recent task history and 0.410 m with random interaction. Which actions generated the history mattered. The hidden variables here belong to the object. [§5, Table 1 and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.11740) |
| **Infer context for a manipulation controller.** *PACE* (October 2026 preprint) and Ghadirzadeh et al., *Bayesian Meta-Learning for Few-Shot Policy Adaptation Across Robotic Platforms* (2021). | PACE encodes recent Cartesian state, actions and rewards, then distills a controller. Ghadirzadeh et al. hold a task sub-policy fixed and infer a platform latent using five matched expert motion demonstrations on the target. | PACE tests held-out arm families, including UR5e, with a five-arm source set including UR10e. Ghadirzadeh et al. report weak excluded-family transfer despite hundreds of simulated geometries. A successful action produced from an expert target demonstration and one produced from self-issued practice have different information requirements. [PACE, §§III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19141v1) · [Ghadirzadeh et al., §§IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03697) |

## The closest VLA comparison is Yang, Finn & Sadigh

*Cross-Controller Transfer via In-Context Behavior Adaptation* (October 2026 preprint) asks how a generalist policy can pool demonstrations from controllers that give commands different meanings and reward different execution strategies. The reported hardware experiments use one WidowX. Four source tasks, six controller types, four gains and 30 demonstrations per cell yield 2,880 trajectories. The authors jointly train the π0.5 policy and context encoder. Their full system receives nominal controller metadata plus behavioral context, typically three demonstration trajectories under the deployment controller, sampled into 128 commanded/achieved transitions with visual context. They encode context before the evaluation episode, and it can come from another task. Their paper therefore already establishes the plausibility of history conditioning, context reuse across episodes and controller-dependent behavior in a VLA. [Yang et al., §§IV-A–D and V](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39280v2)

Their most informative result for us is the comparison **within the same held-out task–controller combinations**. With zero target-task demonstrations under the deployment controller, nominal controller conditioning achieved 12% success, their system-identification baseline achieved 27%, and behavioral context from experts on a different task achieved 50%. The context can communicate both the response of the plant and a strategy an expert chose under that response. In a separate test, context from tool-bit insertion gave 35% success on USB insertion with the same compliant controller. The authors do not claim that a source-trained adapter can repair a separately frozen VLA on a new physical arm after a single unlabelled, self-issued probe. [Yang et al., §§VI-A and VI-D, Figs. 4 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39280v2)

**Our question is how much manipulation skill can be recovered when the target arm supplies evidence about its own motion and nobody supplies a target strategy.** A held-out angle on Panda tests the simplest instance. Transfer to an excluded physical arm under its nominal interface tests whether the result covers morphology. A useful failure case is an insertion that requires a compliant search strategy missing from the motor's proposals. A short free-space pulse might identify a command mapping perfectly and still leave that strategy unavailable. Gates and choice of PaliGemma are implementation details. Identifying the conditions under which self-practice rescues native task success would be the empirical contribution. [Paper justification and controls](../../outputs/embodiment-command-frame-20261120-r1/JUSTIFICATION.md)

A direct comparison would keep the downstream motor and adapter capacity fixed and match sensor channels, context length, and initial-state distribution. Give one condition the robot's own prescribed probe and give a separate, explicitly privileged comparator a demonstrator's behavior on a different task under the same target condition. Train to interpret both support distributions on source robots, so poor performance on an unfamiliar kind of support cannot explain the result. Pair both with empty and wrong-condition practice and an analytic fit using the same measurements. If expert context alone rescues the task, the extra information could be the contact it observed or the strategy it conveyed. To distinguish those, compare a non-expert contact probe visiting matched contact states with the expert behavior. These are proposed follow-ups. The active source-only command-frame screen tests a data-allocation decision and uses no expert target context. [Active experiment](../../outputs/embodiment-command-frame-20261120-r1/PLAN.md)

## What the practice data can tell us

Let the hidden condition be the body and its current settings. Suppose two settings produce the same distribution of measured responses under the chosen practice commands. An observer with equal prior odds will retain equal posterior odds after that practice. Sixteen repetitions of an uninformative motion cannot reveal contact friction before contact. Different probes, sensors, or task-relevant contacts can break this ambiguity. Conversely, distinct latent coordinates can encode precisely the same predictions after a change of basis. Testing the sign of a physical response or the correction selected has a clear interpretation even when a coordinate of the learned vector has none. Offline meta-RL work formalizes related ambiguity from the data-collection policy. Meta-learning experiments also show that a query can reveal task identity and let a model ignore its support altogether. [Dorfman, Shenfeld & Tamar, NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/248024541dbda1d3fd75fe49d1a4df4d-Abstract.html) · [Yin et al., ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03820)

For a concrete example, apply +45° and −45° command-frame rotations in the **same** Panda simulation. Keep its appearance and instruction fixed. A positive horizontal pulse yields opposite transverse motion in world coordinates, so both regimes are distinguishable from public action and wrist motion. On an independent query asking for a forward movement, matching practice should select an opposite-signed correction in the two regimes. Supply practice from the other regime to that exact query and check that the predicted correction changes in the expected direction. A model that recognizes Panda from its image cannot solve this comparison using that information alone. This example illustrates an identification test. The [registered scale design](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/SCALE.md) specifies its own training and held-out angles.

## How a hidden estimate reaches our frozen VLA

1. **Train the source motor.** SFT general PaliGemma on successful simulated Panda/Sawyer demonstrations and measure each arm on fresh starts. Freeze one qualified checkpoint. These demonstrations teach the manipulation task. [Source evaluation](../../outputs/libero-paligemma-dualarm-20261120-r2/evidence/source50/native.json)
2. **Pretrain the reader.** Encode a practice history of issued commands and measured motion into a 16-dimensional context. Predict the response to a query command from a separately reset trajectory under the same hidden setting. Cross settings within each arm and reuse the same probe distribution across arms. A predictive target gives the context a job before asking it to help with control. The existing reader is proprioceptive. Visual or contact-dependent identification requires that the relevant signals reach it. [Reader implementation](../../outputs/libero-paligemma-crossarm-20261120-r1/adapt_eval.py) · [Information audit](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/INFORMATION.md)
3. **Fit the reader and adapter together while keeping PaliGemma frozen.** The VLA proposes a seven-dimensional action. In the implemented output adapter, the context produces a matrix and bias for an affine correction to that proposal. The correction goes to the simulator on the VLA's output path. The original VLA receives its usual inputs and weights. For the gated design, bypass the whole adapter when the gate chooses off. At evaluation fix reader, adapter, gate and motor weights. Only the history changes online. The earlier completed joint fit used motion-matching targets after expert collection failed on Sawyer. The later expert-action recipe is documented separately. [Reader and adapter](../../outputs/libero-paligemma-crossarm-20261120-r1/adapt_eval.py) · [Training provenance](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/SCALE.md) · [Hard bypass audit](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/IDENTITY.md)

A close competing approach is *ReGround* (November 2026 preprint). It estimates a target-specific action map and delay from task-independent motor probes, then corrects a frozen VLA's actions. The raw action passes through until a shift alarm fires. On its 250 ms latency sweep, 1,000 target transitions yielded 0.98 success. Its detector fired falsely in 2.1% of nominal episodes. This provides both a simple-control benchmark and a metric for unnecessary intervention. Our proposed learner has a smaller fixed practice budget and must use its source-trained weights on the held-out arm. Give competing calibrators the same public practice and account separately for any extra target data. [ReGround, §§3–4 and Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20244v1)

## What would justify each claim here?

| Claim | Decisive comparison and evidence against it |
| --- | --- |
| Practice reveals a hidden **physical factor**. | Randomize known signed settings within each arm independently of layout and behavior. Fit a readout on source settings and score a new setting, a withheld combination, and a new family separately. Compare with a frame-zero or initial-state predictor. If the initial observation predicts equally well, the study has shown an available shortcut. Privileged labels may score a blinded estimate after inference. |
| The motor **uses** that information. | Hold the query, motor checkpoint, policy noise and probe budget fixed across matching, deliberately mismatched and empty histories. Check predicted motion and the sign of the action correction. A smaller error versus empty alone permits a generic history-dependent correction. A gain that survives matched swaps weakens the identification claim. |
| The information improves **task success**. | First supply the known correct setting through the same available correction interface. If even this controller cannot help, fix the motor or interface. With demonstrated headroom, compare raw, matching, mismatched and analytic control on paired fresh starts with native reward. Report paired wins, losses and uncertainty by arm/shift. Matching a closed-form calibration supports its use on that shift and leaves the benefit of learned inference unresolved. |
| The gate **knows when to do nothing**. | Verify exact action equality when off. Separately report how often the learned gate turns on in nominal episodes, fails to turn on in shifted episodes, and changes native success in each stratum. The archived hard-off implementation reproduced all 19,563 raw controls. Whether it autonomously picks off on a fresh nominal episode remains open. [Replay and audit](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/IDENTITY.md) |
| The learner transfers across **embodiments** or across **attempts**. | Hold out whole physical families. Vary a setting within each arm so a lookup of the arm name fails. Compare current-episode prefix with retained previous practice on the same query. Only a retained-history gain licenses the cross-attempt claim. Prior UR5e pilots make the final target a previously studied family on fresh starts. Lock the protocol using other development families first. [Factory review](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/REVIEW.md) |

The present evidence supports a narrow conclusion. The existing reader improved motion prediction versus zero context on a very small panel. Matching and wrong-arm context were nearly tied. A claim that our reader identified embodiment and improved manipulation on a new arm remains unverified. The [scale review](meta-pretraining-scale.md) gives the least expensive next test.
