# How much diversity does embodiment meta-pretraining need?

**For the Panda–Sawyer study, spend the next simulation budget on an equal-cost comparison of more hidden settings versus more resets per setting.** Published work shows that the diversity of hidden tasks can determine whether a learner uses practice on a new task. The closest manipulation studies also show how poorly a result on new variants of familiar arms can predict success on a new arm family. The literature gives us useful comparison points and experimental controls. A minimum number of demos or source arms for frozen PaliGemma remains unknown. [Algorithm Distillation, Appendix H](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.14215#A8) · [PACE, §IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19141v1) · [Ghadirzadeh et al., §V](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03697)

## Experiments that varied scale

| Primary study | What varied | What the result tells us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laskin et al., *Algorithm Distillation* (ICLR 2023) | Number of distinct hidden key/door placements drawn from 6,561 possibilities. The test tasks were held fixed. The main study also reports 2,000 multi-episode source RL training histories for the Dark environments. A history can contain many episodes. | Training with 1, 9 or 18 placements produced no observed improvement across test episodes. With 37, 75 or 151, improvement emerged but overall performance stayed low. The 1,212 and 2,424 conditions performed best. The appendix does not document equal transition counts across these conditions. This is evidence of a breadth effect under this collection procedure. [§5 and Appendix H, Fig. 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.14215#A8) |
| Bauer et al., *Human-Timescale Adaptation in an Open-Ended Task Space* (ICML 2023) | The generator offered 200 million possible world–game pairs or 25 billion. The same 23M-parameter Transformer configuration received 25 billion environment steps in each pool. | The larger pool improved held-out performance, including at the lower end of the score distribution. Holding architecture and steps fixed makes this a stronger argument for breadth. The pool sizes describe possible pairs. Counting visited pairs or independent examples requires a separate measurement. [§3.5, Table G.12 and Fig. H.4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/bauer23a/bauer23a.pdf) |
| Raventós et al., *Pretraining Task Diversity and the Emergence of Non-Bayesian In-Context Learning for Regression* (NeurIPS 2023) | In a noisy linear regression problem, vary the number of distinct hidden weight vectors available at pretraining. Compare the fitted Transformer with two calculable estimators on fresh weights. | At low diversity the model behaved like a Bayesian estimator restricted to the finite training pool. At higher diversity it approached ridge regression on genuinely new weights. This supplies a concrete alternative explanation for a two-arm reader: it could choose between familiar responses. Test a new continuous value and a withheld combination with different correct actions. [Paper and estimator comparisons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15063) |
| Lee et al., *Supervised Pretraining Can Learn In-Context Reinforcement Learning* (NeurIPS 2023) | Standard Dark Room used 80,000 training examples over 80 goals, with 100 interaction steps per context and an optimal action label for the query. Dataset sizes in the separate sweep were labeled 10k, 50k, 100k and 200k. | The 10k setting degraded performance, with similar results at larger sizes in that sweep. In a separate fixed-goal test the hidden variable was the action mapping. Given 100 offline transitions, the trained model matched the optimal return on all 20 held-out permutations. The second experiment is especially close to hidden command calibration. Its target-time context length and its pretraining corpus size answer different questions. [§5, Appendices A.5, B.2–B.3 and Fig. 9d](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14892) |
| *Collective Learning for Unified Robotic Manipulation via Predictive Dynamics Adaptation* (PACE, October 2026 preprint) | Source-arm sweep of 1 (Sawyer), 3 and 5 arms. Full training covered ten manipulation tasks, with fifty arm–task expert policies. Fig. 4b evaluates the same held-out KUKA iiwa across sweep points. | Reported iiwa success rose with additional arms for PACE. The behavior-cloning baseline declined going from one to three. The full source set included UR10e, and the four held-out arms included UR5e and Panda. PACE trained a state-based controller with demonstrations and online distillation. Its result motivates varying source families and reporting donor relatedness. It gives no fixed-budget minimum for a frozen VLA. [§§III–IV, Table I and Figs. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19141v1) |

## Closest scale anchors in robotics

| Question | Reported scale and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| How much simulation went into a reader explicitly trained to infer hidden physics? | In RMA, 1.2 billion simulated steps trained the base locomotion policy and privileged encoder. A separate 80 million simulated steps trained the adaptation module to predict the teacher's latent from 50-step windows. These are phase-specific interaction counts at one successful operating point. The paper does not sweep the student data volume to estimate a minimum. [Kumar et al., training details](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.04034) |
| Do many synthetic geometry variants cover an unseen arm? | Ghadirzadeh et al. generated 100 variants for each of four arm families. In the excluded-family setting they trained with 300 variants from three families and adapted with five matched target motion demonstrations. The authors judged held-out-family reaching unsatisfactory, even though new variants of familiar platforms transferred better. Count mechanism families and procedural variants separately. [*Bayesian Meta-Learning for Few-Shot Policy Adaptation Across Robotic Platforms*, §§IV–V, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03697) |
| What does locomotion at hundreds of embodiments show? | Ai et al.'s strongest policy trained on 817 simulated embodiments and transferred to real Go2 and H1 robots. At deployment the model received a morphology descriptor computed from each target URDF. This supports training across diverse structures with a known description. It leaves the amount of practice needed to infer a hidden structure open. [*Towards Embodiment Scaling Laws in Robot Locomotion*, §4 and Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05753v2) |
| How much data have robot models used to learn from context? | ICRT used about 2,000 retained DROID trajectories and 1,098 trajectories over 29 tasks in its own multitask corpus. With alternative goals in the same scene, a demonstration provides information about the instruction to execute. In VLRLA's physical Franka two-color study, 169 demonstrations trained a bridge to use feedback about a hidden destination. These are examples of successful dataset design for task inference. Both change what the robot should do while keeping the hardware fixed. [ICRT, §§III–V and Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15980) · [VLRLA, real-robot study](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19517v1) |

## What to count in our experiment

**The historical reader saw eight independent training tapes and 128 transitions.** On each of two arms it saw two 16-step practice tapes and two independent 16-step query tapes, all at nominal settings. Its 3,200 optimization draws reused those tapes. On one new query layout per arm, normalized motion MSE was 1.092 with matching practice, 1.097 with wrong-arm practice and 1.555 with zero context. That tiny matching-versus-wrong difference and the single layout per arm leave body-specific inference unresolved. The motor has a different data requirement: on the 50-start source panel the extended regression checkpoint succeeded on 34 Panda and 20 Sawyer starts. Any downstream comparison needs the same frozen checkpoint across controller conditions. | Proposed source-only fit | Physical families | Hidden command settings per family | Separate practice + query resets per cell | Cost per fit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| More resets on fewer settings | 2 | 2 | 12 + 12 | 96 tapes, 1,536 transitions |
| More settings with fewer resets | 2 | 4 | 6 + 6 | 96 tapes, 1,536 transitions |

Use the same 16-command probe on each cell, the same query distribution, fit budget and three training seeds. Two complementary narrow subsets prevent the choice of an easy setting from deciding the result. A new yaw angle and a withheld yaw–gain combination test interpolation and composition separately. Share test resets across controllers, keep evaluation practices and queries disjoint from training, and report equal-cell paired errors and seed spread. Each query gets matching, matched-wrong and empty practice plus an analytic calibration from the same commands. The planned shared bank costs **256 unique tapes and 4,096 controls total** across train and evaluation. These counts are an affordable discrimination experiment chosen for this repository. [Exact settings, sampling, budgets and advance criteria](../../outputs/embodiment-meta-factory-20261120-r1/SCALE.md)

**What would change the training decision?** If wider coverage helps on both held-out settings and matching practice beats swapped practice, advance to a separate comparison that adds a physical source family at fixed cost. If all histories perform alike, investigate probe informativeness and the training objective. If analytic calibration already captures all the gain, report that result for command shifts and move the learned-method question to a shift where images or contact might add observable information. Require an oracle correction through the same frozen motor interface to improve native reward before spending on that larger trial. Qualify an extra source family and an independent development family before a single locked UR5e panel. Four starts or repeated windows from one reset cannot characterize family transfer. The full design proposes 100 paired fresh UR5e starts and restricts the inference to this task and previously studied robot family.
