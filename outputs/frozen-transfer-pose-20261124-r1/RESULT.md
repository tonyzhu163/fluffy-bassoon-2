# Frozen π0.5 requests arm-specific unreachable poses on moka, while its failed drawer commands are statically reachable

Kinova3’s own moka tapes contain absolute wrist-and-grip goals that its mounted chain cannot attain, even with the full joint range available. Its two failed drawer tapes show a different pattern. Every one of those 600 goals has a static joint-valid full-pose witness. The configuration-dependent wrist change rescued neither drawer and lost two of the three untreated Kinova3 replay successes. **Stop this wrist residual on the tested panel.**

## Which goals were actually requested?

Both arms were then tested on the **identical world target** using their true FK. Panda had a full-pose witness for all 3,059, including every Kinova3 goal. The arm models have different links, joint ranges and torque limits with a common gripper. ## Why test a wrist change on drawer?

Both 7D action vectors specify six normalized OSC components plus one gripper command. At 20 Hz, robosuite scales XYZ by 0.05 m and the world-axis-angle vector by 0.5 rad per component, and left-composes the latter on the starting grip-site orientation. All 3,059 commands had a nonzero rotational vector. Absolute target reconstruction agreed exactly in position and within 2.5 × 10⁻⁸ rad in attitude. Seven torque motors drive each arm and two actuators drive its finger slides. The shared gripper has one command dimension. The OSC fixed gains are 150 and approximately 24.49 per Cartesian axis. Kinova3's first five motor limits are ±32 and final two ±13 N·m; Panda's are ±80 and ±12 N·m. The simulation integrates at 2 ms. [Kinova3 configuration](evidence/replay/libero_goal-3-14-Kinova3.json) · [Panda configuration](evidence/replay/libero_goal-3-14-Panda.json)

For static IK, Kinova3 has continuous hinges 1, 3, 5 and 7 and compiled bounds of ±2.41, ±2.66 and ±2.23 rad on hinges 2, 4 and 6. All seven Panda hinges use their compiled finite ranges. The rate sensitivity intersects these ranges with one period of manufacturer joint travel. Kinova3 joint speeds are 1.3963 rad/s on joints 1–4 and 1.2218 on 5–7. Panda's are 2.175 and 2.610 rad/s on those groups. These manufacturer settings describe hardware; the native OSC uses torque motors and soft positional limits. A few physical states slightly overshot a compiled position limit. All observed net joint displacements over 50 ms stayed within the respective speed envelopes. Instantaneous speed between samples, acceleration and torque feasibility of the IK solutions are unmeasured. The Gen3 manufacturer's own position bounds also differ from this simulator's. ## Matched physical intervention

Three fresh CPU branches per arm/start shared a reset and the complete original tape. Untreated replay reproduced the earlier CPU endpoints, orientation, pinch midpoint and reward. The adaptive branch read the known mounted model, public joint/TCP observations and the current command. It replaced wrist rotation only when a position-prioritizing witness improved the kinematic solution in the 50 ms box. The sham applied the same step-indexed command residual with its sign reversed. On the shared adaptive/sham prefix their component magnitudes matched to within 3.0 × 10⁻⁸ normalized units. Translation and gripper commands stayed bit-identical. Full details of the rule and equal-amplitude action-box scaling were registered before either branch ran. ## Qualification and decision

All source tape digests matched the original frozen-policy job. Every CPU reset matched its prior CPU qualification, including the original released scene/row fingerprints shared with the recording. The CPU and GPU settled full-state hashes differed on all cases. Ten of twelve untreated CPU reward tapes matched the recording exactly. Panda drawer 14 succeeded at 173 versus 175 controls. Kinova3 moka 14 first crossed the inherited trajectory comparison scale at 489 and reached the 496-control tape end without the GPU success. Its first certified exclusion at 404 and its largest measured-angle bound at 415 precede that divergence. For Kinova3 drawer 15, CPU motion agreed through 117, covering first plate contact. Later TCP drift reached 8.17 mm. Drawer 14's maximum TCP drift was 0.000123 mm. Public observations can also lag the integration boundary: public TCP differed from boundary FK by up to 1.67 mm across this panel. The intervention used public observations; the physical scorer read the true integration boundary. [Per-case replay qualification](evidence/readback/qualification.csv) · [Pinned source and receipts](RUN.md)

These data establish arm-specific exclusions on actual frozen commands, particularly late in the moka task that the recorded policy solved. They also falsify static joint infeasibility as the explanation for the two Kinova3 drawer trajectories to the precision reported here. Resolving a one-step wrist/position conflict in the hardware-rate model changed contact trajectories and reduced native success on the fixed tapes. The evidence supports stopping this general correction. A targeted follow-up would require independent failures whose issued commands enter a certified exclusion **before** the failed interaction. Policy replanning after a wrist change, hardware behavior, collision-free paths through the witnesses and a task-appropriate wrist tolerance remain unmeasured.
