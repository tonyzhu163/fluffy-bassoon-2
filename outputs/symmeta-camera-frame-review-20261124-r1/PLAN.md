## Problem and competing hypotheses

Three shipped readers reach 5.83° mean absolute yaw error upright on 64 UR5e OOD traces. Rotating the image alone by ±90° produces 84.71° and 90.11° errors. Rotating issued XY alone has much less effect than rotating reported displacement alone. This is evidence of a visual/local-motion dependency. Digital recovery of an exact permutation measures the implementation. The older eight physical orbit examples were scored with SymMeta on two tasks. [Shipped-reader intervention](../symmeta-perceiver-mechanism-20261124-r1/RESULT.md) · [Physical history](../symmeta-camera-command-20261123-r1/RESULT.md)

H1. Expressing visually observed motion in a calibrated reference frame before learned inference reduces held-out command-yaw error at fixed source data and optimization. H2. Local calibration and sufficient source camera variation let a raw-motion Perceiver recover the same task-relevant geometry. H3. Any observed difference follows from source underfit, support/view shortcuts, or image resampling. I favor a digital benefit relative to late conditioning. I reserve judgment for a spatially conditioned comparator and for physical orbits. Native benefit is less certain because task success can tolerate yaw error, depend on its direction, or be limited by the motor.

## Pre-existing work and a fair comparator

Jiang et al. train behavior-cloning policies to predict task actions from demonstrations. Their Plücker map represents each pixel by a world-frame ray direction and moment. Their scratch visual encoder takes concatenated input channels; in pretrained models separate spatial encoders fuse before pooling. Their matrix-token ablation showed no observable benefit in that experiment. Camera effects also depended on background cues, with scenes designed to break those cues. The paper gives a strong reason to test spatial calibration here. Its task success does not quantify our inverse problem. [Full paper, §§III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02268v1) · [Author site and ray conventions](https://ripl.github.io/know_your_camera/)

Existing test-time view synthesis also aligns observations for a frozen manipulation policy. Claims of first camera conditioning, first viewpoint normalization, or first frozen-policy camera correction have prior art. The narrower possible contribution is evidence about where to place calibration when a source-trained passive-support inverse reader estimates an unseen body's command map for a fixed motor. Its value depends on the comparisons above. [Heo et al., AnyCamVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05868)

A pre-pooling control costs one change to feature construction. Use H from reference to observed pixels. On observed B, append projected reference coordinates π(H⁻¹p) and their validity to the nine local channels. On registered A, give the same grid channels in its own lattice and equal first-layer capacity (3×3×3×48 = 1,296 additional kernel weights per arm). Keep the global H as in the registered pair. This tests local access to the same supplied planar calibration while leaving observed motion on B's lattice. A true Plücker comparison requires per-view K, R, center c, pixel convention, shared reference calibration and any plane available to A. Concatenate d=normalize(R K⁻¹p) and c×d to local channels before the scratch CNN, after applying the exact crop/flip/downsampling transform. For A sample that same observed ray field at the coordinates used for its warped features, with equal validity treatment; reindexing rays does not change the camera that emitted them. For synthetic orbit training retain generator K/R/c or the actual ray field in a common reference frame. A general 2D H alone does not supply unique metric rays. If only H is available, name the planar grid control accurately.

Reuse the same source bank, per-step draws, PR7 architecture/hyperparameters/losses, seed count, 2,500/5,000 source selection protocol, scorer and physical keys. Report if common checkpoint chosen for the token pair or selected prospectively for the spatial pair. Freeze the choice before targets. Cache ray fields by unique camera geometry and apply the same locality mask as the motion features. Start from the same fresh parameter distribution; a shared widened first layer makes parameter count identical. This is a small run-local feature/first-convolution change.
