# A bowl-trained robot visual reader transfers a correction to bottles

With 16 bowl practice tapes as its entire fitting set, the frozen π0.5 vision tower selected the successful bottle retry on 9/12 strict two-zero cases. The identically fitted general PaliGemma PT-224 tower selected 6/12. Swapping the camera histories between executions from the same start reduced the robot reader to 3/12. A separate check using six established public gripper and wrist features reached 10/12 with the same 16 source tapes. The robot tower transferred some information about the individual execution. These results give no performance advantage over that compact motion control. [Paired reader comparison](summary.json) · [Motion controls](motion-controls.json)

The first four arms were registered. The six-feature comparison was added after those results were available and used features published in the earlier project run. They measure jaw widening during commanded closure, mean and variation in closed-command jaw width, vertical wrist range and horizontal wrist travel. The matched control used the identical 16 fitting tapes, pooled class centroids and distance rule, with each channel scaled on source data. The older 64-tape neighbor model reproduces its published 64/96 on the newly executed branches. All six readers use an explicit feedback rule. A positive reward identifies the attempted destination, and two zeros favor the less likely completed destination. [Prior physical controls](../libero-interrupted-practice-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

## What changed on transfer

The source had eight clean successes and eight dropped failures. We fit a single completion model pooled across the two bowl destinations. No bottle data entered the fit. On bottles the general tower called all 24 practices failures at its source threshold. The robot tower recognized five of eleven successful practices and nine of thirteen misses. The selected destination depends on the relative scores of two attempts, so a correct choice can accompany a poorly calibrated completion probability. Neither visual representation establishes general semantic recognition of arbitrary placement failures. [Robot scores](robot.json) · [General scores](vlm.json) · [Pixel scores](rgb.json) · [Motion scores](motion.json)

Of twelve cyclic pose-and-prompt-order groups from six physical reset IDs, six met the strict criteria. These used four different query starts, and each supplied two opposite hidden assignments after the same two zeros. The robot reader solved both sides of three groups and one side of the other three. General PaliGemma solved one side of every group. Across all 96 assignments the robot reader gained four successes over the general reader and lost none, with one gain apiece on query poses 26–29. All four came after two zero rewards. The physical trials and assignment rows are dependent.

With knowledge of the hidden goal, the executed query options could reach 80/96. A hindsight selector given the exact practices and reward bits could reach 70/96, compared with 64/96 for one restricted to poses, order, issued commands and observed rewards. The latter can match the best aggregate reader score here while necessarily tying at 6/12 on the matched strict cases. This is why both outcomes matter for interpretation. ## Physical evidence and paper decision

Baige CPU replayed 36 bottle branches using archived π0.5 controls, with 12 sham practices, 12 D18 practices sharing the same commands and 12 independent nominal query tapes. The nominal practice and query states and predicates matched their archives throughout. D18 moved the bottle on all twelve branches at controls 49–146. All twelve failed. Eleven of twelve sham practices and ten of twelve queries reached the prompted goal. There were no off-target hits. Rendering used llvmpipe and public agent and wrist cameras. Simulation took 137 seconds at `8d10794e`. The four primary readouts finished at `5c51d09d`, with bounds at `145850d9` and the secondary motion checks at `758c3522`. Zero GPU-hours. Full tapes remain on Baige at `/mowenjie/fluffy-bassoon-2/outputs/libero-witness-transfer-20261119-r1/cpu01`. [Physical branch records](branches.jsonl) · [Tape hashes](tapes.sha256) · [Plan](PLAN.md)

Keep π0.5 fixed as the motor and retain general PaliGemma plus the compact motion control when testing a learned update rule. On bowls, the last dual-camera observation already solved the strict contrast. On bottles, the cases share the same event family and their physical outcomes were known from the earlier D18 study. This qualifies a controlled demonstration of how to interpret a zero reward. The next discriminating benchmark would hold both terminal evidence and reward fixed while verifying that an earlier observation changes which retry works. A held-out event family would test generalization beyond drop recognition. These results provide no official LIBERO-Pro2.0 score and leave the update rule itself unlearned.
