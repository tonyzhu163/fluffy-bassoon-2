# How much meta-training teaches a policy to use hidden dynamics?

For the frozen PaliGemma plate-pushing study, the closest published test is DPT’s **fixed goal with an unseen action mapping**. The strongest evidence for buying more *distinct conditions* comes from AD and AdA. Their experimental units differ, and none estimates the number of robot families or demonstrations required for Panda/Sawyer-to-UR5e transfer.

## Four primary comparisons

| Study | What the authors varied or collected | What was held out and what happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lee et al., Supervised Pretraining Can Learn In-Context Reinforcement Learning** (NeurIPS 2023). The repository credits DPT correctly. | Dark Room had 100,000 context–query–oracle-action examples, each with 100 random interaction steps. **80,000 examples from 80 goals** trained the model. The other 20,000 came from 20 disjoint goals. Fig. 9d separately labels a **10k / 50k / 100k / 200k** sample sweep. The authors report degradation at 10k and similar performance above it. Its 100k label refers to the original corpus size. | On the 20 held-out goals, DPT achieved **61.5** average return given random context that itself averaged **1.1**. A separate dynamics test fixed the goal and permuted command meanings. With **100 support transitions**, DPT matched the optimal return of **83** on all **20 held-out permutations**. The permutation result is one operating point. [§5.2, Apps. A.4–5 and B.2–3, Figs. 4a, 7 and 9d](https://jonathannlee.com/files/lee2023supervised.pdf) |
| **Laskin et al., In-context Reinforcement Learning with Algorithm Distillation** (2022 preprint, ICLR 2023). | The main experiments collected **2,000 multi-episode source-RL learning histories** in the Dark environments and **4,000** in Watermaze. A separate breadth sweep varied the number of distinct hidden key/door location pairs in a space of **81² = 6,561**. | On the same held-out pairs over **300 evaluation episodes**, training on **1, 9 or 18** pairs yielded no observed in-context improvement. **37, 75 and 151** yielded some improvement with weak overall performance. **1,212 and 2,424** worked best. Appendix H establishes a task-count effect under that collection recipe. It does not document matching the transition budget across counts. [§§4.1 and 5, App. H, Fig. 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.14215#A8) |
| **Bauer et al., Human-Timescale Adaptation in an Open-Ended Task Space** (AdA, ICML 2023). | The XLand 2.0 curriculum could sample **200M** world–game pairs (4k worlds × 50k games) or **25B** (50k × 500k). Both pools received **25B training steps** at each of two Transformer sizes, 23M and 75M parameters. Pool capacity counts possible pairs. Realized unique visits were unspecified in this comparison. | **1,000** procedural test tasks excluded every training combination of goal and production rules. The larger pool improved held-out adaptation, especially the **20th percentile** and the larger model. This is a matched-step, two-point breadth comparison with one initialization per ablation. It gives no fitted task-count exponent. [§§2–3.5, App. G.11, Table G.12 and Fig. H.4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/bauer23a/bauer23a.pdf) |
| **Shi et al., VLRLA** (2026 preprint), physical Franka hidden-goal task. | **185** successful demonstrations of placements into two plate colors, **169** for fitting and **16** for validation (8 per color), **6 chunk targets per episode**. Paired trials reused an identical first attempt with opposite reward labels and supplied a different continuation accordingly. | Both colors were familiar. Held-out source episodes and **44 physical evaluation runs** tested whether feedback guided the next attempt. Success rose after first feedback. This is a single corpus size for a binary hidden goal on one arm. Its pairing demonstrates how to make feedback informative with shared source trajectories. [§6.2, Fig. 7, App. A.3 and Table S5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19517v1#S6.SS2) |

## Other scale evidence and checks on the repository review

- **Task families versus instances.** Raparthy et al. varied **2k, 10k, 30k, 100k** procedural levels of one MiniHack task and separately **1, 8, 12** training task types. Across five unseen tasks and three seeds, one-shot returns plateaued around 30k levels, with little further benefit beyond eight types. The prompt was an expert demonstration. In Procgen, Cobbe et al. fixed PPO at **200M steps** per fit and varied **100–100,000 levels per game**. Closing the train/test gap on new levels of the same game took up to **10,000** levels. Procgen measured zero-shot generalization. Original XLand reported one final-generation scale of **3.4M experienced tasks and 200B steps per agent** in its author summary. [Raparthy et al., §6, Fig. 6b,d](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/raparthy24a.html) · [Cobbe et al., §3.1, Fig. 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/cobbe20a/cobbe20a.pdf) · [DeepMind, XLand results](https://deepmind.google/blog/generally-capable-agents-emerge-from-open-ended-play/)
- **DPT and AD.** The review’s DPT counts check out. Visual Miniworld used **32k training / 8k validation examples**, 50 context steps each, and the same four color goals at test. AD’s history totals are in **§5** of the cited version. The separate task-count sweep above is more informative for deciding whether to collect additional conditions. [DPT, App. A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14892#A1.SS5) · [AD, §5 and App. H](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.14215)
- **Robotics references.** Fu et al.’s ICRT retained **~2,000 DROID trajectories** after grouping an initial 10k sample, then used **1,098 trajectories across 29 tasks** with competing goals in each scene. Across held-out tasks on the same robot setup, Table II gives **0.0%** average success for DROID alone, **73.3%** for the multitask corpus alone and **79.2%** for sequential training on both. Corpus structure, domain and size vary together. VLRLA’s separate RoboTwin collector lists **204,800 nominal chunk slots** before masking per run in **App. A.2.1**, correcting the review’s A.1 pointer. Its six seen/six unseen task split and separate fruit archive (**391 episodes, six subtasks, 4,692 chunk decisions**) also check out. Vintix II Table 1 confirms **209 training tasks, 10 domains, 3.8M episodes, 709.7M timesteps**, including **45 Meta-World tasks, 670k episodes, 67M timesteps**. The authors reserved 46 tasks. These are operating-point counts. [ICRT, §§IV-A and VI-B, Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15980#S6.SS2) · [VLRLA, §5.1 and App. A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19517v1#A1.SS2) · [Vintix II, §3.1, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05112v1#S3.SS1)

**Decision for Panda/Sawyer → UR5e.** The first reader fit had **two source arms, eight training trajectories and 128 transitions**. At fixed trajectory count, vary persistent command mappings within *each* source arm and compare with adding layouts at existing mappings. Hold out a full mapping on both source arms. At a matched query, switch only the support between settings that require different corrections. If correct support fails to beat wrong support and a fixed correction on fresh layouts, extra labels from the same trajectories give little reason to expect useful hidden-dynamics inference. Reserve UR5e for the frozen comparison on native success. The review’s 256/1,024/4,096 trial tiers are local planning choices.
