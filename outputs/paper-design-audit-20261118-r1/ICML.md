# What should a frozen robot change after a failed attempt?

**My recommendation for the ICML paper is to study how a frozen policy learns whether to change its target, change how it moves, or retry.** A zero reward after placing a bowl on the wrong surface is evidence about the intended destination. A zero after dropping it before reaching either surface says little about the destination. A robot that treats those signals differently could save a physical attempt. The question is whether that decision transfers to fresh poses and held-out tasks and improves native success over an explicit rule using the same evidence.

## A baseline the original experiment missed

I rescored the 16 archived RoboCasa compact-bowl development cases using one rule. Repeat the attempted side after reward 1 and switch sides after reward 0. I looked up the selected physical query branch and the assigned goal in the saved native outcome matrix, using the same query scene as the archived decoder.

There were eight physical supports, each scored against both possible goals. The query branches used a privileged scripted carry. The two timeout cases are the same tape under two goals, so they cannot estimate performance on execution failures generally. ## A first experiment with existing benchmark assets

Use **LIBERO-Pro2.0 Goal 4 and Goal 8**, both in the fitting split. In one the bowl goes on top of the cabinet, in the other it goes on the plate. At the pinned release, all four BASE/S20 definitions have identical parsed object, fixture, region and initial-condition declarations. The two destinations have distinct native goal predicates. This makes a custom ambiguous-goal experiment possible without inventing kitchen assets. Physical reset parity, predicate exclusivity and frozen π0.5 competence for *both* destinations still require native rollouts. The standard benchmark uses the released instructions. [Cabinet task](https://github.com/LIBERO-Pro-Team/LIBERO-Pro2.0/blob/e5500c690c1498eca1b7050350654964190cdf70/assets/bddl/libero-pro2.0-1680-v1/libero_goal/00_base/BASE__put_the_bowl_on_top_of_the_cabinet.bddl) · [Plate task](https://github.com/LIBERO-Pro-Team/LIBERO-Pro2.0/blob/e5500c690c1498eca1b7050350654964190cdf70/assets/bddl/libero-pro2.0-1680-v1/libero_goal/00_base/BASE__put_the_bowl_on_the_plate.bddl) · [Split](../libero-meta-learning-20261117-r1/SPLITS.json)

1. **Qualify the decisions.** Start from a common reset bank. Under normal and S20 timing, execute each destination with the native control strategy and a candidate timing correction developed on fitting scenes. Include the default action. Compare an informed selector with the best fixed execution strategy on fresh cases at the same control budget. Evaluate independent noise draws for branch ranking and scoring. A history learner needs two reachable placements and an execution change that helps under identifiable conditions.
2. **Build the trials.** Independently sample a destination and timing condition, and retain both across a support attempt and a fresh query pose. Give the selector one neutral instruction and the same two destination options in every trial. The motor can receive the instruction for the option the selector actually chose. Keep private the assigned goal, task-file identity, object-of-interest list, hidden timing and unchosen outcomes. Log public camera views, proprioception, issued commands and feedback. End support at a common public boundary, then score its behavior against both native predicates. A timeout that satisfies neither predicate gets zero under both assignments. Treat the two labels of one tape as a single dependency block.
3. **Fit one decision-pretrained selector.** Train offline across trials to predict which executable continuation succeeds from the exact query state, using native outcomes for supervision and retaining tied choices. Keep all weights fixed at evaluation. Train the current-episode-only control on the same labels and action menu. Compare against native retries, repeat-after-success/switch-after-failure, and a stronger rule that reads public placement evidence and estimates lag from command–motion history. The stronger rule should retain goal uncertainty after a miss. Charge each arm the same support and query controls.
4. **Measure the value of experience.** On paired fresh resets, report attempt-two native success over *all* trials with retained versus cleared support, and each policy's absolute success. Test whether changing the reward on the same completed support redirects the query destination, and whether changing support motion at matched reward changes the execution strategy. Use naturally ambiguous timeouts as a check on unjustified goal switching. Include relevant and different-condition donor histories, independently fitted seeds, and autonomous support before making an own-experience claim.

## What would earn the headline

The fitting-task pair is a mechanism test. Keep the registered eight-task LIBERO-Pro2.0 panel for transfer under released conditions. To claim transfer of **both** goal and execution adaptation, also test fresh ambiguous task pairs with every constituent goal excluded from selector fitting, and qualify their motor choices. A useful bonus test uses an earlier attempt at a different manipulation under the same robot timing. A one-delay win alone supports timing adaptation. If the simple grounded rule matches the learner, report how much it explains. If even knowledge of the condition fails to improve the frozen motor, expanding the history corpus has little diagnostic value.

Prior work already uses sparse feedback from attempts, transfers VLA histories between rollouts and conditions robot behavior on earlier command–motion examples. The potential contribution here is to **show when an identical failure signal warrants different corrections, train from the measured consequences of those corrections, and establish the benefit on physical task success under equal interaction budgets**. [Watch, Try, Learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03352) · [VLRLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19517v1#S5.SS4) · [Cross-Controller Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39280)

## First experimental readout

Across four fresh LIBERO bowl pose pairs, pacing the first attempt raised native retry success from 11/16 to 14/16 and identified the correct destination on all 16 assignments. A learned command–motion lag threshold tied the always-paced retry at 14/16 and reached the goal sooner on normal timing. Prioritize learning how to obtain informative feedback on attempt one. Carry the fixed paced exploration and retry policy into the transfer panel as the strongest comparator. [Experiment and controls](../libero-bowl-decisions-20261118-r1/RESULT.md)

## First-attempt transfer to bottles

A bowl-trained policy chose the alternative destination first (plate on bowl, rack on bottle) and adjusted its first-attempt cadence from public motion. On six fresh bottle pose pairs it achieved 14/24 native retry successes, compared with 14.5/24 expected for balanced always-pacing. A retrospective cabinet-first probe identified all 24 destinations and reached the paced query motor's 16/24 ceiling. For the next paper panel, train goal-conditioned estimates of which exploratory placement will succeed, include several fitting pairs, and select reserved pairs with an executable retry and a scene-dependent choice of informative probe. Preserve balanced pacing and the strongest source-fitted constant as baselines. [Transfer study](../libero-first-attempt-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

## What two practice attempts buy

On four fresh pose IDs across four LIBERO layouts, all six familiar goal pairs showed a reversal in which placement succeeded. Choosing the first target from the scene yielded 53/64 native retries against 52/64 for the fitted fixed target on the two prespecified pairs. Across all six pairs both scored 157/192. A reward-blind retry scored 81/192, and a two-entry repeat/switch rule recovered the full reward advantage. In an exploratory three-goal replay, decoding two rewards earned 154/192 against 105/192 for decoding one under the same control budget. Allowing the second probe to depend on the first reward added zero on the fitting set for every triple, so the simpler fixed schedules were selected.

For the ICML study, use a fixed exploration schedule with a fitted reward decoder as a baseline at each interaction budget. The next discriminating experiment is a paired execution failure and wrong-destination failure that both produce reward zero. Measure on fitting tasks whether retrying the same target with a different motion succeeds in the first case and switching targets succeeds in the second. Expose the same candidates, rewards and public trajectories to the learned reader and an explicit completion-aware rule. Freeze them before testing fresh tasks. Report the paired native query success as the primary outcome and goal inference and query motor reachability alongside it. [Scene study and three-goal replay](../libero-scene-probes-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

## Does a zero reward call for a different correction?

A paired bottle replay resolves this for the current four-action menu. A bowl-trained motion classifier recognized only one of eight physical placement misses and earned 28/48 native retries. A simple repeat/switch policy with an earlier bowl-fitted cadence threshold earned 31/48. This reached the per-tape upper bound, including 17/32 after zero reward. Seven of eight misses still included a bilateral grasp and an 8–34 cm lift. A failure to place the bottle on the rack is a poor proxy for a failed grasp.

Keep this bottle panel as a mechanism control. For a positive ICML test, establish that a different **executable correction** can recover a failure under the same reward and interaction budget. Start with a known rack destination on fitting poses and measure a transport or release correction alongside the existing two cadences, both of which scored 0/6 on the delayed rack queries. Use native placements to identify cases in which the appropriate correction actually differs with public motion history. Carry the simple lag and reward rule and the upper bound conditional on each observed tape into the reserved comparison. [Failure diagnosis and upper bounds](../libero-failure-diagnosis-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

## A motor correction that recovers delayed placements

On three bottle evaluation poses, both existing cadences missed all delayed rack placements. After observed lift, replacing the remaining motor actions with those from a successful fitting demonstration recovered 2/3, both still successful at the final control. The same correction also reached 3/3 under normal timing. Applying it every time scored 5/6 across timings, the most any selector could earn from the two measured branches. A prior-attempt lag rule scored 4/6. On six other poses with a paced opening, always correcting scored 7/12 versus 5/12 with lag routing. Treat the correction as a fixed motor baseline. To establish across-attempt ICL, build a fitting panel where the persistent condition changes which executable correction succeeds, then freeze that panel design before collecting reserved poses and tasks. ## A prospective moving-rack control

LIBERO-Pro2 D02 moved the rack about 7.5 cm around lift. Across twelve previously unused pose IDs the stationary and moving worlds looked identical at reset. A source-fitted first attempt earned reward on 8/12 stationary and 0/12 moving racks. Its reward redirected four moving queries successfully but lost four stationary ones, tying fixed nominal replay at 9/24. A current-image correction reached 13/24 and the live frozen VLA reached 18/24. In hindsight, the live VLA plus all tested corrections could earn only 19/24. Keep this case to test whether history use translates into a physical advantage over reactive control. For the primary benchmark, first measure the live motor and its recoverable failures on fitting tasks, with the action decision placed before the new trial reveals the hidden condition. ## Can the current attempt infer timing soon enough?

On eight LIBERO-Pro2 development tasks, BASE and S20 had identical reset observations. Public wrist and gripper traces first differed at control seven. S20 returned the wrist observation from reset. On fresh poses a controller using an earlier attempt to choose cadence scored 11/16 against 9/16 for fixed default. Issuing the first ten commands from the frozen VLA's proposal and then choosing cadence from the current wrist trace matched all eleven successes with no practice requirement. Five of eight delayed tasks failed under every measured schedule. Use this reactive policy alongside fixed baselines in the timing panel. The experiment distinguishes when prior experience reveals a condition from whether that earlier information improves native task success. ## When is another practice attempt worth taking?

Use native success on a fresh pose as the outcome and the number of physical practice attempts as the cost. The hidden-goal control asks the same frozen motor to try candidate instructions. A positive reward can settle which goal to pursue. A failed attempt leaves two explanations open: the attempted goal was wrong, or the robot missed it. A transferable learner has an opportunity when it can tell which of those failures justify spending another attempt.

On the existing development panel combining two new goal identities with one familiar identity, a small source-trained reader and a three-entry reward table both scored 798/1,152 native queries. The same reader scored 350 when its reward bits were masked. The table achieved the same 798 when it skipped the second practice after a first success, saving 284 of 1,152 second attempts. Spending the same expected number without using the first reward scored 734.4. These are paired replays on four pose IDs. A secondary source-fitted rule that allocated second attempts among just half the failures earned 680.8 expected successes, compared with 679.4 for ranking by the second probe’s predicted reachability and 669 for random allocation among failures. The simple reachability control accounts for most of that apparent advantage. [Protocol and comparison arms](../libero-hidden-goal-transfer-20261119-r1/PLAN.md) · [Mixed-goal query scores](../libero-hidden-goal-transfer-20261119-r1/mixed.json) · [Stopping comparison](../libero-hidden-goal-transfer-20261119-r1/stopping-mixed.json) · [Practice allocation](../libero-hidden-goal-transfer-20261119-r1/value-mixed.json)

For the paper, show a curve of query success against practice attempts and compare the learned policy with reward-triggered stopping and with public reachability at matched cost. Keep the released LIBERO-Pro2.0 scores alongside this custom hidden-goal experiment. Prior work already uses previous robot interactions to improve the parameters of a manipulation policy. The narrower question here is whether a learner trained on other tasks can value a physical attempt before paying for it, using sparse native feedback. [Van der Merwe and Jha, CoRL 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/merwe25a.html)

On the prospective triple of drawer opening, bowl-to-stove placement and plate pushing, all three goal identities were new to the reader. The learned reader and table each scored **230/288**, compared with **96/288** when the reader's reward bits were masked. Skipping a second practice after a first success preserved 230 and saved 27.1% of second attempts. Spending the same practice budget independently of reward scored 209.4. Averaging over every distinct support/query pose order preserved these rates. When only half the failed trials could get more practice, the learned allocator scored 193.5 against 194.5 for simple reachability and 192 for random allocation. [Prospective comparison and cost curves](../libero-hidden-goal-transfer-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

Use this as the paper’s diagnostic for sparse-feedback transfer. The native motor could succeed on only 234/288 assignments, leaving four successes for a stronger goal reader to recover. For the next fitting panel, collect public trajectories after a wrong placement and an unsuccessful placement that earn the same reward. Execute both available corrections on the next reset: change the destination, or keep it and change the motion. Measure how often the better correction reverses across the two histories before scaling a trajectory reader. A learned advantage on held-out tasks at matched cost, against the fitted table, reachability and a completion-aware rule, would support a stronger ICML contribution.

## Can identical zeros require opposite corrections?

A new 96-episode experiment found a fitting witness. Two practices tried the cabinet and then the plate from identical starts. Both earned zero. When the first missed and the second reached its target, switching to the cabinet succeeded on the fresh query. When the first reached its target and the second missed, keeping the plate target succeeded only after changing from five to ten controls per replan. On four new poses the strict reversal appeared in zero of eight prespecified groups, and in zero of 48 when all distinct pose orderings were considered.

The reader identified all 256 fresh goal assignments. Substituting a different execution of the same instruction from the same initial state preserved every choice. Restoring the original motor cadence raised native success from 160 to 224/256 for both policies. With these goals, noise draws rarely changed completion at a fixed fresh start. For the next fitting benchmark, vary a recoverable execution interruption independently of destination, record the actual trigger, and measure whether matched-start executions require different corrections. Compare the resulting policy with the same-reset donor and the untouched motor before spending the official task split. ## Learning how much to trust a failed attempt

An independently crossed practice interruption produced a transferable history effect. On four fresh bowl poses after two zero rewards, the bowl-trained reader earned 341/512 native queries versus 274.5 for an independently reassigned execution history from the identical starts. On six bottle poses with a live frozen motor, it earned 72/96 across all rewards versus 62 for a reward table, 65.5 with reassigned histories and 67 using only issued commands. The live motor itself recovered three placements that continuing the nominal command sequence missed. A single jaw-opening feature outperformed the learned reader on an earlier open-loop bottle subset. Include that simple comparator. [Paired interruptions, live recovery and reader controls](../libero-interrupted-practice-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

The same bowl estimator transferred to the released D18 bottle-displacement mechanism with an identical approach and live post-drop replanning. D18 fired on all twelve practices. The reader earned 73/96 fresh-query successes, compared with 60 for the reward table, 69.5 for reassigned histories and 72 from issued commands alone. Use this as a development result for interpreting sparse feedback. It tests destination choice, with a custom two-goal protocol on release assets. For the main paper, freeze matched-start interventions across multiple task families and record actual triggers, then evaluate on fresh tasks with command-only, same-start donor, explicit completion and unchanged-motor controls. Report native success alongside goal inference and motor coverage. A 13-success reward-only comparison and a one-success comparison against commands justify different claims. [Released-event readout](../libero-interrupted-practice-20261119-r1/released-live.json)

## Which benchmark can measure a better retry?

A bowl-trained reader that combined commands and sensed motion reached 192/288 native successes on three fresh LIBERO-Object layouts. Commands and rewards alone reached the same 192, the best available outcome from the two query instructions. Motion changed 38 choices, all on assignments where either choice earned the same reward. The 288 assignments reused 108 live episodes. On the soup layout both prompts placed soup. On the salad-dressing layout both prompts reliably placed their own object, so the two practice rewards already identified the goal. On the ketchup layout, always asking for ketchup reached the motor ceiling. These layout effects explain why a change in inferred goal or a gain over a reward table alone is insufficient evidence for motion-based adaptation. [Matched native queries and layout-level results](../libero-object-history-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

For the ICML experiment, train the retry decision on same-object placements where a failed practice can mean either a missed grasp or a completed placement at the wrong destination. On fitting scenes, execute the alternatives from identical query resets and record which succeeds after reward-matched histories. Train a small decision-pretrained reader on those measured outcomes. Keep the frozen motor's original instruction and default execution available. Freeze the menu, reader and baselines before evaluating fresh task identities. Report paired native success at equal interaction budgets for rewards alone, commands plus rewards, the completion-aware rule and the full reader. Include the native branch ceiling for every layout and retain unreachable cases in the reported scores. The Object panel supplies a negative control for transfer. The earlier bottle advantage over commands justifies testing the mechanism on new tasks. [Decision pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14892) · [Bowl and bottle evidence](../libero-interrupted-practice-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

## Which timing signal earns another successful placement?

Directly fitting native retry advantage on four source bowl poses reached 66/96 on live bottle practices with a gripper interruption and 71/96 with released D18. Inferring practice completion first reached 73 and 75. Averaging the fitting query labels over the four source poses improved the direct reader to 73 and 72. For decision pretraining, pair each practice history with several independent query starts so its label estimates how useful a retry is across resets. Keep a completion-then-goal-inference baseline. [Direct, intent and completion comparisons](../libero-retry-value-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

Opening the gripper just after lift versus during descent gave the benchmark two physically different consequences for the same original command sequence. A reader that added wrist motion around jaw opening earned 237/320 native successes against 224 for a reader using jaw timing and the same public summaries. Histories involving a single practice whose intervention never triggered supplied 14 net additional successes. The trigger-matched subset contributed one net loss. Among 256 assignments where early and late openings actually fired on both practices, jaw timing earned 182, wrist timing and motion earned 181, and the same readers with independently reassigned motion earned 160 and 160.5. Include both the unconditional result and this mechanistic contrast. The experiment worth scaling is a frozen reader learning how much evidence a failed attempt provides about the retry goal when motion timing and success can vary independently. Vary transport speed on fresh task families and score the retry with a live motor. [Matched early and late perturbations](../libero-retry-value-20261119-r1/RESULT.md) · [New start and query panel](../libero-release-phase-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)

A direct test of the cost of counterfactual labels on the timing menu gave 18.25/32 native successes for one logged retry per history and 18.00/32 for fully paired retries at the same 48-episode budget. The simple lag table reached 20.25 with either training dataset. In the next mechanism, collect a stationary practice and one randomized retry per fresh history, plus a prespecified paired subset to quantify how often retry choice matters. Select a mechanism with measured room above a simple history rule before increasing collection.
