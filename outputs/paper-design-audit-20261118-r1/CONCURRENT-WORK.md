# Can we train a frozen robot policy to learn more from each attempt?

Research and proposed evaluation, 19 November 2026. This is a targeted review of primary papers available by this date. Recent preprint findings are author-reported. The proposed training comparisons below have yet to be run as a common benchmark.

## What should transfer

Given the same new task, current observation and motor, does an offline-trained transformer use one or two rewarded attempts to choose a better action on the next reset? The transferable object is the rule that maps experience to a change in the policy. A pretrained VLA supplies visual recognition, language grounding and executable actions. Freeze its original weights throughout. Meta-train a small history-to-action module across robot tasks and freeze that module at deployment too. Any measured improvement applies to the combined system. If the module only selects between two instructions, the demonstrated capability is instruction selection.

Li et al. formalize a transformer acting on a prompt as a learning algorithm. Their regression experiments test it against explicit estimators and vary the tasks seen in meta-training. For robots this suggests benchmarking the *update* against an explicit inference rule and varying task diversity independently of rollout count. Their generalization bounds use assumptions about data and stability. An adaptive policy deciding which robot interaction to collect changes the distribution of its future evidence. The published rates therefore cannot simply be applied to our closed-loop robot returns. [Li et al., Sections 3–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.07067)

## A learning process that would produce interpretable evidence

1. **Construct a distribution of learning problems.** Each trial has a persistent hidden property that is unavailable at the query reset. Use at least two distinct problems: an ambiguous goal under unreliable execution, and a known goal under an unknown control condition. Reuse a fixed frozen motor or a mixture of fixed exploration strategies to generate ordinary attempts, including failures. Cross support and query poses and independent motor noise. Include histories that agree in reward and attempt number and imply different advantageous actions at the same query. Include histories where the outcome is genuinely uninformative.
2. **Give the policy physical choices worth learning.** Begin with a small menu of coherent, executable motor actions, including the native policy. Within fitting tasks, measure the effect of the candidate action from the same query reset under each hidden condition. A context-aware controller needs both evidence about the condition and a choice whose success depends on it. Report how often those two conditions hold, including on the full reserved panel.
3. **Vary the supervision while holding the interface fixed.** First train a DPT-style reference on good query actions from paired native returns. Generate multiple independent query continuations when ranking stochastic branches. Train on tied actions as ties and never expose branch outcomes, future frames or privileged state in the input. Next train a DIT-style value model using only the action actually logged and its outcome, then weight supported decisions by their estimated advantage. Suboptimal attempts remain in context even when their actions receive low imitation weight. Keep the reader, action menu and test-time proposal budget matched. Use a separate improving-history data arm for genuine AD and a plain next-action fit on the static logs. Charge for DPT's branches, AD's history generator and DIT's data coverage. [DPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14892) · [DIT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/dong25d.html) · [AD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14215)
4. **Give each mechanism a way to fail.** Independently fit the current-episode-only verifier using identical training cases. Also train with a mix of empty and retained histories so clearing at test time has a meaningful interpretation. Use physically recorded, same-start alternative executions to replace motion at matched command and reward where possible. Vary feedback on the very same physical placement only where both feedback values correspond to a permissible hidden goal. Compare with reward-only switching, a public completion/lag rule, and an evaluator that knows the hidden condition but must use the same motor. Let a current-prefix controller see what it could acquire during the query at the same charged cost.
5. **Measure learning and its price.** Establish zero-context competence, then score a fresh native query after 0, 1 and 2 attempts. The primary comparison uses the same query states and shared support trajectories for every reader. Report the gain of retained experience over the separately trained current-only verifier and the absolute success of both. Compare learning curves at equal physical-control budgets and report cumulative successful tasks or attempts to first success with failures at the budget limit retained. Separately evaluate autonomous support choices before claiming learned exploration. Report inference latency, candidate calls and any test-time gradients for an online adaptation baseline. Include the strongest fixed or RL-trained policy at matched deployment cost.
6. **Test transfer at the level of tasks.** Use the predeclared LIBERO-Pro2.0 task split for execution adaptation. The hidden-goal protocol built on LIBERO is a separate custom diagnostic. Keep the held-out identities sealed through model selection. Vary source task/condition diversity at fixed total practice episodes and update count, separately from increasing histories per task. Report paired task-level results and individual fits, including regressions. Thousands of combinations of a few poses do not create thousands of independent tests.

## How to decide which paper the evidence supports

If a current-only verifier gains as much as the history learner, the benefit is better offline action selection. If a repeat/switch or completion rule matches the trained transformer across new tasks, the explicit rule explains the observed improvement. If history improves performance at matched cost, changes decisions in the expected direction under controlled evidence swaps, and that effect transfers across held-out tasks, we have evidence of learning a portable update rule. If DIT-style training also approaches the DPT-style reference with fewer physical training branches, we have an additional claim about the supervision that rule needs. The latter is a hypothesis and depends on coverage of effective actions in the offline logs.

The current bowl pilots justify those distinctions. A phase-aware reader scored 237/320 compared with 224 for jaw timing plus summaries. On the 256 assignments where both release timings fired on both practices, the comparison was 181 versus 182. Reassigning the same-start execution evidence dropped the respective scores to 160.5 and 160. That is evidence that the factual execution matters and a warning against inferring an advantage for the richer reader from the aggregate. These assignments reuse five support poses and four query poses. [Physical results](../libero-release-phase-20261119-r1/RESULT.md)
