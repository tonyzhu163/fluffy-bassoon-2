# A steerable carry made the previous reward useful on two development pairs

The frozen Human300 π0.5 grasped a fruit in all eight compact-table episodes. Six episodes put a fruit inside the right bowl and none put one inside the left. Three of those six met the native hand-far (>25 cm) release criterion: one of four left requests and two of four right requests. No scene met the predeclared requirement that both explicit instructions succeed. The collector exited normally and never ran the four reserved scenes. This custom one-fruit goal uses native predicates; no episode solved the original four-fruit task.

Two retrospective continuations isolated the release failure on 961001. After exact replay to first bowl entry, a fixed open-and-retreat action produced a right-bowl reward at control 429 instead of timing out (left prompt; entry 400) and at control 400 instead of timing out (right prompt; entry 372). This recovered releases without correcting destination choice. With the original frozen commands, at H350 six of eight had grasped, four had entered a bowl and one had released; at H500 those counts were eight, six and three.

**What the feedback readout means.** Apply a fixed two-attempt rule to the physically executed scripted alternatives: try left on support; if rewarded repeat left, otherwise try right on its paired query scene. Use the same hidden preference on both scenes, score both preferences on each pair, and compare to constant-left, constant-right, and reversed feedback with identical query trajectories. Correct feedback gets 3/4 query goals, either constant choice 2/4, and inverted feedback 1/4. On 961000 → 961001 support timed out for either goal, so all rules obtained 1/2. On 961002 → 961004 a successful left placement made the reward informative, so correct, constant and reversed feedback obtained 2/2, 1/2 and 0/2. Support reward was 1/4 in all cases. This is an illustrative readout on two development pairs with a hand-written selector and privileged carry controller, not learned adaptation or held-out evidence.

**Next experiment.** Distill the post-grasp carry and retreat into a small controller that sees only camera images, gripper state and a requested side. The VLA remains frozen. First demonstrate left and right releases on new matched scenes without simulator coordinates or grasp triggers. Then train a selector across support/query pairs and compare real, cleared and inverted reward histories on withheld scenes. On a failed pickup, reward alone is ambiguous; the observed placement or a no-placement signal can tell the selector whether it learned anything about the goal.
