# What did measuring the second retry buy?

A transformer trained from one randomly logged retry per history averaged 19.0/32 native successes on eight development tasks. Revealing both measured alternatives on the same fitting histories raised that to 19.5/32. The extra 32 training episodes bought half of one development success on average. At equal 48-episode budgets, collecting one retry on 24 histories and both retries on 16 histories gave 18.25 and 18.00 respectively. The signed difference changed direction across the logging draws and initializations. The experiment establishes that the one-label fit is feasible. The development panel has insufficient resolution to establish equivalence or choose an optimal collection ratio.

## What was collected and what was hidden

We reused 32 fitting cases on eight task IDs and 32 cases on eight different development IDs from LIBERO-Pro2.0. Each task has two support/query pose pairs crossed with BASE and S20 timing. The frozen π0.5 policy used default cadence for every practice. A fresh query reset was shared by the native five-control cadence and a paced alternative. Each branch logged its own native success. Neither learner used PPO, GRPO, a teacher, or an improving sequence of policies. All 320 original episodes already existed, including the other two query modes excluded here. The costs in this report count the supports and the selected query modes that a prospective collector would have needed. Ten development cases failed both retries, nine succeeded at both and thirteen distinguished the candidates. Across the complete panel the native motor succeeded 18/32, pacing 13/32, and a selector given the best measured branch could reach 22/32. The existing lag rule reached 21/32. With one label per case on all 32 fitting cases, replacing the factual support with the other timing's support dropped the transformer's mean from 19.0 to 15.5. Its advantage over the separately trained current-only model was only 0.75. Sensitivity to support and a practical advantage from it answer different questions. ## What if only some histories get a second retry?

On the same 32 histories, buying a randomly chosen second retry on one history per task cost 72 training episodes and averaged 19.00/32 successes. The corresponding one-retry score was also 19.00. At a fixed 48 episodes, 21 supports, 21 logged retries and six alternate retries averaged 18.375/32 (18 to 19 across fits), compared with 18.25 for all single retries and 18.00 for all paired retries. This mixed allocation used 12,613 controls on average. The separately fitted current-only model reached 18.25 and the same model with history cleared reached 18.75. These small differences offer little basis for choosing a branching fraction on performance. Randomly auditing a subset still buys a direct measurement of branch disagreement.
