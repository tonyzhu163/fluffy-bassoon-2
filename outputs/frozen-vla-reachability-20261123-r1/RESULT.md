# Native arm geometry changes which approaches work

**Test object and phase selection from public observations first. Compare a joint-limit-aware wrist correction as a second intervention.** Kinova3 loses access to particular demonstrated wrist poses, and changing wrist orientation can remove a link collision. The two failed frozen-policy drawer trials broke down before the bowl-release poses that show the clearest geometric difference.

## What failed on the drawer

The separate native benchmark ran frozen π0.5. We used its saved raw actions on the same released rows, one CPU replay per arm and layout, and observed the contacts at the end of each 20 Hz control. Cross-host settling produced different full-state hashes. We measured replay agreement at every saved TCP and finger sample and every saved hand-orientation boundary. Contact partners and fixture motion below come from the CPU reenactments. The recorded source of each action tape and its digest are in the per-case records. [Native episode results](../frozen-vla-native-transfer-20261123-r1/RESULT.md) · [Replay measurements](evidence/drawer-replay.csv)

- **Drawer 14.** Kinova3 pulled the top drawer 160.6 mm. Its fingers contacted the drawer on 200 of 300 control endpoints and never contacted the bowl. The smallest margin to a finite arm-joint limit was 0.756 rad. Its TCP matched the recorded target trajectory within 0.00013 mm throughout, and its entire reward tape matched. Panda contacted the bowl at control 123 and lifted it 285 mm in replay. The Panda replay succeeded two controls earlier than the native 175-control run. The benchmark's successful Kinova tracker and both successful translation corrections independently establish that this layout is solvable on Kinova3.
- **Drawer 15.** In CPU replay Kinova3 first contacted the plate at control 67. Through control 117 its measured TCP, finger slides and chunk-boundary hand rotation remained within the inherited 1 mm / 0.01 rad comparison scale. It had made no drawer contact and retained at least 0.371 rad of arm-joint margin through that prefix. Later replay drift reached 8.17 mm at the TCP and 9.38 mm in one finger. The CPU drawer stayed closed for all 300 controls, and both hosts timed out. The late CPU trajectory briefly exceeded a soft joint limit. It cannot explain the earlier visit to the plate. Panda opened the drawer and contacted the bowl on control 117 in its successful replay.
- Neither replayed Kinova failure registered a moving-arm link collision with the scene or self at a sampled control endpoint. Finger and hand contact was common. Contact between endpoints, contact forces, perceived object identity and the frozen motor's intent are unobserved. A camera-only change, grip-target error or dynamic end-effector response could each affect where the first interaction occurs.

## Where geometry matters

For each task we took human Panda demonstrations 0 and 1 from the original LIBERO archive. Their actual fixtures and movable poses differ from the native query resets. We reconstructed each donor's contact frames from its saved full physical state and XML fixture poses. We then transported the recorded approach, first finger contact and last carried-object contact into the paired native scene. Pick poses follow the starting object, release poses follow the receiver (including drawer articulation), and a previously placed pot follows the stove. Original fingers and bounded fixture coordinates are projected onto their native ranges. Both arms see exactly the same staged object poses. These are privileged queries. **A geometric bound separates a missed IK search from a genuinely excluded pose.** Pick one point along each hinge axis. Each point belongs to the links on both sides of that hinge, so distances between adjacent points are invariant. Their sum bounds the distance from the first point, fixed in the base, to the last, fixed in the hand. For a requested TCP pose we subtract the maximum motion of the last point allowed by 0.01 rad of wrist rotation. The remaining excess over chain length is a lower bound on TCP position error. Optimizing the points can tighten the bound. Every choice of points gives a valid inequality. The stored offsets, distances and FK rigidity checks reproduce the result independently of IK convergence. ## Architectural decision and falsification

Use the two public camera streams, instruction, recent decoded Cartesian proposals, measured end-effector and jaw trajectory, and robot joint proprioception to infer the next interaction. The first diagnostic fork should distinguish releasing an opened drawer and approaching the bowl from continuing to pull, and approaching the drawer handle from approaching the plate. A pose-conditioned residual can then use public joints, the robot's known kinematics and limits, and the proposed tool pose to select an attainable nearby approach. The observed same-position tilt solutions and alternate-posture contacts provide candidate supervision in a separate development set. An arm-specific constant translation would not encode an object change or distinguish the two release attitudes.

Scene body identities, fixture articulation, ground-truth object frames, collision distances, IK labels, and successful human poses from the same evaluation layout family are privileged offline diagnostics. At deployment, object and phase estimates would have to come from the public observations. Using source-only demonstrations and independent target practice, compare raw actions, a fixed correction, a pose-only residual, and a task-space policy that can change the approached object or phase. Reuse one source of action proposals and paired resets/noise within the native job. Measure intended-object contact and progress as diagnostics, and decide on native terminal reward including losses on previously solved starts.

Deprioritize the planner if fresh failures consistently select the correct object and phase and a pose-only correction recovers their native rewards as often while preserving existing successes. Deprioritize the pose residual if failed proposals already admit arm-clear poses, if they never request the excluded geometry, or if improving pose accuracy repeatedly fails to improve native reward. The exact exclusions here establish real local differences. A failed solver, a failed tracker, or either single donor route cannot establish task impossibility. The witnesses are static configurations sampled at a few contact phases. They establish neither a continuous collision-free approach nor a stable dynamic grasp.

The earlier UR5e calibration pilot showed that better free-motion prediction can accompany lost native successes. An earlier Sawyer plate experiment showed that changing actions at first contact could rescue some failures and spoil successes. Neither isolated these Kinova cases. This study used two human donors per task, three purposively selected tasks and two layouts each. The two donors and five solver starts measure sensitivity to the chosen motion and initialization. The six selected layouts support case comparisons, with two observations per task for assessing variability.
