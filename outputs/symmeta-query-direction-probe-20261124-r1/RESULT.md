# A lateral calibration pulse improved held command and motion predictions on bounded general maps

On four official LIBERO tasks shared by simulated UR5e and IIWA, a lateral pulse during a separate 15-control attempt reduced error on the independent frozen motor tape. Averaged over axis swaps and general maps, the physical reader’s target-command RMS error was **14.97 mm-equivalent**, compared with **30.69** for an equal-effort along-line pulse and **34.29** for passive calibration. On the same maps, its forecast of actual five-control grip-site motion missed by **8.00 mm**, compared with **11.71** along-line and **12.08** passive. Both lateral improvements had the same sign on all four paired task clusters. The general-map comparison supports a matched reader experiment. The swap-only result remains uncertain. ## What changed during calibration

Each layout-11 baseline used the three archived frozen-command means held for five controls apiece. Original sub-chunk commands were unavailable. Within each arm, start and map, all five schedules shared the first five physical controls and their camera and encoder responses. The sign and axis were computed from the completed first chunk. The second and third chunks used opposite signs, with the same causal headroom limit for both axes and both sign orders. Every issued and simulator-bound z, wrist and grip component was preserved. All eight starts were retained. [Causal generator and map draws](design.py) · [Example physical support with issued and actual target controls](cpu02/UR5e-libero_10-5/general-orth+.npz)

The source-derived requested amplitude was 0.23317 normalized units. Both pulses fully cancelled on four of eight arm–task starts. On the other four, the third chunk had less headroom for the cancelling pulse. Mean XY translation then shifted by 0.00344–0.01463 units per control. Among the four balanced starts, the held-map lateral-minus-along differences were −19.45 mm-equivalent for command and −4.34 mm for motion. Among the four imbalanced starts they were −9.49 and −2.74. Each subset covered three task identities. [Balance, realized pulses and held estimates](cpu03/summary.json) · [Per-start amplitudes](cpu03/scores.json)

Sign-averaged issued XY energy per attempt was 8.673 for both active schedules and 8.246 for passive. Across all families, average actual simulator-bound XY energy was 5.438 along-line and 5.447 lateral. Headroom scaling avoided clipping. Exact contact with an issued XY bound occurred on 80/960 along-line controls, 40/960 lateral controls and 0/480 passive controls. A descriptive check restricted to the three arm–task starts with zero bound contacts on either schedule still favored lateral by 26.29 mm-equivalent and 4.52 mm against along-line. Those starts spanned only two task identities. The mean weak/strong singular ratio changed from 0.057 passive to 0.073 along-line and 0.241 lateral. ## What the oracle and finite pulses establish

All injected matrices had maximum absolute row sum at most one. The general subset included two orientation-preserving and two reflecting draws, each shared across arms. In every stored holdout, pulse and support checked by the scorer, measured relative OSC XY goals followed the injected convention within **3.4 × 10⁻⁹ m**. Independent ±0.5 axis and zero-XY branches on layout 14 measured finite five-control response. Across starts, native x-response magnitude was 42.61–50.45 mm per normalized unit and native y-response was 48.51–49.41. Zero XY still moved the tool 0.14–1.17 mm while z, wrist and grip followed the same baseline. The largest held-map deviation between a measured response direction and the injected column was 3.45°. Displacements include finite-time servo dynamics. Repeated random noise draws were outside this experiment. [Both measured direction columns, drift, asymmetry and actual control maps](cpu03/readback.json)

The diagnostic that observed actual target setpoints recovered held commands to within **0.000015 mm-equivalent** on every fit, including passive. The recorded supports had sufficient mathematical rank for this noiseless calculation. Lateral excitation helped when the map had to be inferred through the arm’s physical response. The earlier broad fixed probe worsened forecasts on all three target arms. This result narrows the prior to a causal, balanced XY perturbation of an existing calibration attempt on bounded planar interfaces. It leaves end-task reward, updated motor proposals, unrelated native controller families and arbitrary embodiments open.
