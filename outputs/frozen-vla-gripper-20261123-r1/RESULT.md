# Native arms share gripper transduction; the approach to the grasp differs

**A positive scalar on the seventh π0.5 action cannot change finger actuation in this benchmark.** Panda, Sawyer, Kinova3, IIWA and UR5e all mount the same simulated PandaGripper. The passive fitting prefixes contain zero commands to close it. The actively excited traces show closure but almost no cross-arm difference in free space. We therefore left all π0.5 weights frozen and trained no onset predictor. We separately measured where the pads arrived and tested a signed lateral offset at closure. [Original archive counts](evidence/cpu01/archive-summary.json) · [Physical pulse measurements](evidence/cpu01/pulse-summary.json)

## What the native controller does

On Baige Linux CPU, we instantiated all five actual arm MJCFs in the native LIBERO-Pro2 BASE drawer-and-bowl case, libero_goal/3, layout 11, seed 31810311. UR5e had six arm joints and the others seven. All shared the same gripper MJCF (SHA-256 `d9d68123909d6910488310d0fae4e358ed4ac831c6d0957f3e30893a5412baac`), signed slide joints and position actuators. The measured force limits were ±20 N per finger. Every 50 ms policy control invoked the real action formatter 25 times. At each 2 ms integration step the sign incremented the two normalized targets in opposite directions by 0.01, clipped to ±1. The affine rescaling gives a 0.2 mm increment per finger per substep until saturation. Zero holds the actuator target while the fingers can continue moving. We checked the actual formatter outputs and actuator control values on each inner step.

With zero arm commands, each arm received 12 close, four zero and 12 open controls twice, at magnitudes 1 and 0.125. Within each arm, all observed finger coordinates, actuator endpoints, pad-center trajectories and task rewards were identical between magnitudes from matching full-state resets. No external finger contact occurred. All five crossed the midpoint of the mechanical 80 mm stroke on closing control seven (350 ms sampled at 20 Hz) and reopening control seven. The largest cross-arm difference from the Panda aperture trace was 0.0000066 mm in this one free-space test. The numerical agreement is conditional on this common reset and pulse. It does not measure delay under a loaded grasp. [All per-control readbacks, original support identifiers and hashes](evidence/cpu01-traces.tar.gz) · [Native instrumentation](native.py)

## What the original π0.5 data can identify

We read both original NPZs for each of 192 nominal source supports (Panda/Sawyer, 24 training tasks, four starts) and 24 nominal held-out supports (Kinova3/IIWA/UR5e, eight official tasks, layout 11). Every passive trace covers the first 15 controls. Among its three bounded five-action means, the least-negative value across all tapes was −0.99111. For five inputs in [−1,1], even one zero or positive command implies a mean of at least −0.8. Thus **all 3,240 passive controls were open commands**, with 0/648 net shrinking chunks. The archive proves the sign of every action in this particular window, but a general chunk cannot resolve the within-chunk onset time. Longer task trajectories do close the fingers, as the drawer replay below illustrates. [Per-arm command bounds and measured jaw changes](evidence/cpu01/archive-summary.json) · [Archive scorer](archive.py)

The matched active supports did excite the jaw: 216/216 tapes used five close, five open and five close commands. The median first closure was 26.50–26.51 mm across arms. On the eight matched held-out tasks, the maximum between-arm endpoint spread at controls 5, 10 and 15 was respectively 0.0034, 0.0209 and 0.0159 mm. These are extrema over the complete specified panel. The repeated command schedule supplies little diversity for learning a task-conditioned response. An equal-input positive gain and identity induce exactly the same sign sequence. A delay or reversal could change that sequence, but the records supply no arm-specific free-space delay to compensate. ## Where the pads arrive on a complete frozen task tape

At equal signed slide positions of +20 and −20 mm the center of the two collision pads lay 3.600 mm along negative tool Z from the OSC grip site on every body. Source-target differences in that offset were below 4 × 10⁻¹³ mm. Subtracting the target's fixed CAD offset from Panda's therefore yields the identity command correction. Using the Panda rigid offset to *locate* the pads reduced mean position error relative to treating the grip site as their midpoint. It did so on Panda itself. Time-varying contact and unequal finger coordinates can still move the actual midpoint.

We replayed one complete recorded frozen π0.5 solution to the native instruction “put the black bowl in the bottom drawer of the cabinet and close it”, libero_10/3, BASE layout 1. Each arm received the same bounded commands until native success or the end of the 206-command donor tape. The first positive command was 75. The terminal horizon is 520; an unfinished tape is censored at its last action. Coordinates and contacts below come from fresh MuJoCo kinematics and substep contact records. The public control interface received only the recorded command sequence.

## Source-only offset comparison

We used both archived BASE layouts of the same native drawer task (1 and 4), on Panda and Sawyer. At the first positive gripper proposal, three branches deferred it by exactly one five-control replan interval. One held the current public TCP pose, and two aimed the pads toward opposite fingers by one quarter of the observed aperture. The controller used the public position, hand quaternion, finger positions and last gripper command. It kept the jaws open during the displacement, then resumed the exact source tape. Identity issued the original tape immediately. All inserted controls counted. A unique signed offset had to exceed both identity and the identical-duration hold in source native completions to warrant evaluating it with live inference. This choice was specified before the fork outcomes. [Source-only selection and controls](GRASP-PLAN.md) · [Executable controller and qualification](grasp.py)

All four branches of each arm/layout matched in full reset state, all recorded prefix rows and simulator state immediately before intervention. The geometric branches changed physical placement. On Panda layout 4 the finger-2 direction recovered the success lost by the hold alone, but the same direction lost layout 1. Each fixed direction lost one identity success and gained none. All Sawyer branches made finger–bowl contact and exhausted the source tape. These four paired cases share one task and two layouts. They support a source-only stop for these interventions; they do not estimate a population success rate. **Replay qualification.** On both layouts the independently instantiated released Panda and current cross-arm Panda had identical full reset hashes and all seven public observation hashes. Compared with the historical GPU archive, the initial camera and full-state hashes differed on both layouts; all five public proprioception hashes matched. The two archived native reward sequences and termination counts reproduced exactly, and the maximum per-coordinate public TCP difference across both unmodified replays was 5.98 × 10⁻⁸ m. The replay clipped archived proposals to the benchmark action bounds for execution. The data do not localize the historical full-state hash discrepancy. Camera observations were disabled after reset during fixed-command CPU replay. No fresh VLA inference or closed-loop target success rate was measured. [Independent reset parity and per-layout errors](evidence/cpu02/fork-summary.json) · [Historical-tape validation](evidence/cpu01/drawer-summary.json)

**Decision. Keep the gripper command unchanged on the native benchmark and spend zero GPU-hours on these adapters.** A constant CAD correction cancels, a same-sign scalar cannot affect actuation, and neither causal lateral offset beat identity. Selection used only the four Panda/Sawyer reward comparisons. Target outcomes, simulator contacts, object poses, compiled actuator state and FK were read for mechanism and scoring only. A source-validated pregrasp depth estimate from public images could test whether a missed approach is recoverable; UR5e's single high approach motivates that question, while Sawyer's contacted failures require a different explanation. The two CPU stages took 126 and 242 wall seconds with a two-core affinity limit.
