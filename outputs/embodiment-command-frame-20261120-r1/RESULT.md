# More command settings gave small prediction gains and little sensitivity to the practice donor

**The source-only breadth screen missed its registered advance rule.** Across three paired optimizer seeds, expanding the training bank from two to four hidden command settings per arm reduced mean held-out motion error by 3.75% for an unseen angle and 4.01% for an unseen combination. The rule required at least 10% on each setting, with an improvement in each seed and a matching-history benefit on both arms. One seed reversed each breadth gain. The scientific job succeeded on Baige as `job-vp5n0hpy8jbj` with zero GPUs. A prior attempt failed at EGL import before issuing a command. **What was measured.** The native simulator returned 256/256 tapes of 16 controls, on 40 distinct placement blocks shared across conditions. All eight first-block direction and inverse checks passed. The training union contained 192 tapes. Each fit consumed 96; the held-out panel contained 64 separate support/query tapes, with eight paired layout indices per family and setting. Nine CPU reader fits used 100 updates each. The score is normalized world-frame wrist displacement MSE. Both arms appeared during training. PaliGemma proposed no actions in this experiment. **Did the reader use which mapping it practiced?** Providing a history improved predictions relative to a zero embedding. Changing the donor to the other held-out setting yielded almost the same result; on Sawyer the wrong donor scored slightly better. As a secondary diagnostic, measure the shortest angular change in the fitted translation-only inverse for a requested positive world-X movement when the donor is swapped. The two ideal command inverses differ by 45°. The median change across three broad fits, two arms and eight paired layout indices was **0.21°** (mean **0.39°**). The inverse is derived from the learned response head; no execution of those inverse commands enters this statistic. This supports an interpretation in which most of the prediction gain comes from a correction common to both held-out settings. It leaves body identification unestablished. **Analytic control.** A linear least-squares model fitted to the same 16 support transitions scored 1.01784 with matching and 1.04937 with swapped support, averaged over the four evaluation cells. This estimator fit an intercept and seven command coefficients for each output. The reader additionally learned a shared motion model across source tapes. Its lower absolute MSE therefore gives no evidence that it identified the withheld map more accurately. The eight preflight checks established a physical signature under a designed pulse sequence. How well the short mixed-action evaluation histories identify the map remains open. [Per-layout analytic scores and fit diagnostics](evidence/native02/fit/analytic.json) · [Pulse and mixed probe designs](COLLECTION.md)

**Decision.** Stop the planned scale expansion at this budget. The study provides a controlled null on using practice to distinguish these two command mappings. It cannot establish native task success, strategy adaptation, or transfer to new arm geometry. UR5e remains untouched in this run. A separate follow-up could compare the existing mixed probes with deliberately informative signed pulses and a low-dimensional, lag-aware calibration before adding any new family. For the comparison with Yang et al., this result illustrates why an experiment must demonstrate sensitivity to the identity of the donor even when history improves average prediction.
