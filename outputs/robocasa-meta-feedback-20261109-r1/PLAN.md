# One-attempt VLA feedback pilots

Use frozen native JAX Human300 π0.5 on Baige, one GPU per job. Native motor produces H50 chunks with ten denoising steps and executes five controls before replanning. Collect the first four development scene pairs for the bowl task in one allocation limited to one hour. The independently launched drawer calibration study supplies the first manipulation check. The sustained approach variant below is available if that study finds its single closure pulse ineffective. All three questions use completed simulator trajectories. Track cumulative allocation across retries, within 50 GPU-hours. CPU development verified on November 9 that both native tasks instantiate, that the arm uses achieved-position OSC in base coordinates without an interpolator, and that the drawer has collision geoms on its handle.

**Calibration.** Hidden approach bias ±2.5 cm along robot base Y. Apply it once per newly computed approach target within 16 cm of the handle, from an open approach through its first closing command. Privileged distances control only the simulated intervention and evaluator. The motor sees the normal images and proprioception. Target updates are based on the achieved pose. Native reward and two-finger contact are scored independently. Signed miss is the lateral fingertip-site displacement from the collision-geom center after the first qualifying closure. Missing closures count as unsuccessful acquisitions and have no signed miss. Each sign gets a support, then a different query scene with zero, inverse and reinforcing correction. The two inverse arms also test native outcome repeatability. Preserve pre-offset actions, before/after public states, front/wrist frames every replan and native outcomes. Test simple history prediction and cross-task transfer once corrective authority is found. Transfer retains or independently redraws the offset on another handle task. Offset-only results apply to the custom condition.

**Hidden goal.** In native PortionFruitBowl scenes the fixed public task is to put one fruit into a bowl. Wrap the task with a hidden left/right preference, using RoboCasa's object-in-receptacle and gripper-far predicates at the first released placement. The original four-fruit task outcome is recorded separately. Goal identity is absent from the motor observation. The high-level policy can supply one of three fixed skill prompts (left, right, either). Replay a completed support under each hidden preference, with identical frames, duration and action and a single binary reward. Evaluate the selected second skill using its independently executed query rollout. Keep failures, including timeouts without placement. Estimate fresh-query success for memoryless, reward-conditioned and reversed-feedback controls. Both goals are evaluated for every scene.

**First-attempt strategy.** On the same trial design, fit discrete policies by maximizing measured total success across support and query. Enumerate the three possible first skills and three choices of second skill for each possible support reward. Compare to a policy selecting the highest first-attempt reward, with its continuation optimized on the same source scenes, plus the best static policy. Record support and query controls and success separately. Presampling real trajectories for every action permits paired policy evaluation because the hidden goal persists, the simulator resets independently, and the only cross-episode input to this selector is the previous skill and reward.

Pair indices 0–3 are development, 4–7 are held out. The same pair may be used under alternative strategies; pair, rather than trajectory, is the sampling unit. Stop escalating a domain if feasible strategies or informative outcomes are absent on development. A one-pair result qualifies the mechanism and identifies failures; it cannot establish a transfer or learning effect.
