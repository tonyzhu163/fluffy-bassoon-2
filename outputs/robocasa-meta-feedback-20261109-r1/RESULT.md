# Frozen VLA feedback pilots, November 9

**The tested bowl prompts gave the history controller no successful second action to choose.** On four development query scenes, the frozen π0.5 motor executed all three alternatives from paired initial states. None of those 12 physical rollouts placed and released a fruit in either bowl within 350 controls. This bounds every selector built from these particular rollouts, including one given the goal and outcomes in advance. It leaves the value of experience on controllable tasks open.

**Drawer correction moved the gripper but produced no handle acquisition or drawer opening on the two examined query tapes.** The separate drawer run closed its first collector when motor predictions diverged before intervention. Its [revised collector](../robocasa-drawer-calibration-20261109-r2/PLAN.md) used fixed nominal action tapes with a ±4 cm pulse as the gripper first approached the handle. On query scene 910211, opposite uncorrected pulses changed signed miss at closure from 6.03 to 7.86 cm. Neither inverse, uncorrected nor reinforcing pulses produced native success or two-finger acquisition under either sign. Only one of eight scheduled query scenes finished all six arms in that collector. Its next scene failed the initial state/pixel comparison.

We independently recovered **physics-only** evidence for that second query, 910213, from its saved native action tape. CPU neutral replay matched all 600 saved pre-action physical hashes. Each of four shifted replays matched all 182 saved hashes through the intervention at control 181, then ran to 600 controls. All five net shifts (0, ±4, ±8 cm) yielded zero successes and zero acquisitions. This checks the mechanics and does not qualify pixel equivalence or closed-loop adaptation. The original collector's GPU usage is accounted in its own run. [First query and collector exit](DRAWER-READOUT.json) · [Independent second-query physics results](DRAWER-CPU.json).

We stopped before held-out bowl trials, drawer controller fitting and cross-task transfer. A next development experiment would put both bowls within the same reachable workspace and qualify each explicit target skill through placement **and release** before training memory. For drawer transfer, first identify a development grasp where the known inverse actually restores native success.
