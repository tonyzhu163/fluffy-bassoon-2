# SymMeta training and reader capacity

**Planar training.** We trained the seed-41 SymMeta reader on 1,344 passive first-attempt traces from BASE scenes (672 each from Panda and Sawyer, 24 tasks, four initial states, seven transforms). Each trace contained three five-control chunks. The transforms were identity and six single-factor shifts (yaw +90°, −90° or 180°, planar gain 0.65 or 1.55, or inverted gripper polarity). All three heads used the same traces and their simulator-assigned transform labels. The per-trace loss was
\[
1-(\hat c\cos\theta+\hat s\sin\theta)
+(\hat\ell-\log g)^2
+\operatorname{BCEWithLogits}(\hat h,\mathbf 1[s_{\rm grip}>0]),
\]
where the normalized pair \((\hat c,\hat s)\) encodes yaw, \(\hat\ell\) is predicted log gain and \(\hat h\) is the polarity logit. We averaged over the batch with unit weight on each term. We initialized the reader and sampler with seed 41, drew 64 traces uniformly with replacement per update, and saved the final parameters after 3,500 updates. AdamW used global gradient-norm clipping at 1, weight decay \(10^{-4}\), and a cosine learning-rate schedule starting at \(10^{-3}\) with a floor of \(5\times10^{-5}\) at step 3,500. The headline Perceiver used the same training data, loss and optimizer protocol. **Visual and proprioceptive heads.** For each 64 × 64 image pair, the CNN received nine channels: horizontal and vertical DIS flow, flow magnitude, signed change in channel-mean intensity, root-mean-square RGB change, horizontal and vertical gradients of the temporally averaged intensity, and two pixel coordinates. Three stride-2 convolutions used 48, 96 and 96 channels, with a two-convolution residual block between the second and third. All convolutions used 3 × 3 kernels. Two spatial softmax pools produced a concatenated 192-vector projected to 96 features, with SiLU activations in the encoder. A 96–192–96–5 MLP predicted raw planar motion, a confidence logit and two parallax coefficients. Before weighted yaw alignment, we subtracted local vertical displacement in centimetres times the sum of the predicted coefficient vector and a learned global two-vector. Valid chunks received weight softplus(logit) + 0.0001. The yaw loss supplied the only task supervision for visual motion, confidence and the vertical projection, through alignment. The gain MLP (5–64–64–2) used each axis’s signed command and local displacement, their absolute values and stabilized log-magnitude ratio. The gripper MLP (4–48–48–2) used the gripper command, jaw-width change, final jaw width and negative command times width change. Each MLP predicted a value and a pooling weight. The gain head pooled over valid chunks and axes, the gripper head over valid chunks. Their respective trace-level losses supervised the pooled values. **Three-dimensional experiment.** We trained a separate seed-41 reader on 960 active Panda/Sawyer probes (24 tasks, two initial states, ten transforms, six signed-axis bursts of five controls). The same visual encoder fed a 96–128–64–4 MLP that directly predicted a three-vector and a confidence logit per chunk. The three-dimensional branch bypassed planar parallax subtraction entirely, although its checkpoint serialized the shared configuration default `use_dz_parallax=True`. A shared per-axis gain head pooled over chunks and scaled each issued command component by its estimated gain (clipped to [0.25, 4]). The rotation estimate was the determinant-corrected SVD of the weighted sum of visual-vector × scaled-command outer products. All heads used the same probe labels, minimizing the sum of squared rotation-matrix error \(\|\hat R-R\|_F^2/4\), mean squared log-gain error across the three axes, and gripper-polarity binary cross-entropy. Visual vectors and confidence received supervision through the rotation estimate. Training used 3,000 AdamW updates, batches of 64 drawn with replacement, and cosine decay from 0.0015 to a floor of 0.000075, with the same clipping and weight decay as planar training. **Perceiver capacity.** Counts include the image encoder, attention blocks, positional and latent embeddings, normalization parameters, biases and output head. The headline and scaling w192 models differ by eight learned positional slots (1,536 parameters). The larger scaling model increases CNN width, latent count and depth as well as attention width. Its total parameter count is **5.315×** that of scaling w192.
