# Does calibrated image registration improve a camera-augmented Perceiver?

Registered on November 24, before fitting or scoring the proposed pair. The prior evidence concerns three frozen PR7 refits. Their mean yaw error on 64 UR5e OOD supports increased from 5.83° upright to 85–90° under quarter turns with physics held fixed. Prior input interventions implicate the relation between visual motion and reported Cartesian displacement. A fixed-camera benchmark cannot decide whether a calibration token suffices. ## Hypothesis and competing outcome

Under independent camera and control changes, resampling a calibrated observation into the reference frame before extracting motion should reduce held-out yaw error compared with learning that operation inside an equally sized Perceiver. Both models receive the same calibration, commands, reported displacement and observed image pair. Our working prior favors a digital benefit, with lower confidence for a physical orbit because depth variation and occlusion violate the single-plane model. The alternative is that augmentation plus explicit calibration suffices.

The primary contrast is A minus B in absolute circular yaw error, averaged equally across displayed rolls −45°, −20°, +20°, +45° on each of 64 compositional/continuous UR5e–π₀.₅ supports, then across the three seeds and eight tasks. Negative favors A. We will favor explicit registration if the paired whole-task interval supports a reduction, directions are reasonably consistent across seeds/angles, and loss on nominal controls and source adequacy do not explain the result. If B catches up after matched training, or the task interval spans meaningful gains for either arm, we will weaken the prior or leave it unresolved. Compare errors to same-support upright, shipped baselines and the effect of incorrect calibration to judge magnitude. A loss on physical orbits relative to B limits the claim to digital changes, even if digital evidence favors A. A physical advantage needs authentic paired observations and evidence on more than the two original task clusters for a broad claim. A null with an improving, poorly fit B cannot distinguish architecture from optimization. We will state the task count, data limitations and all measured comparisons.
