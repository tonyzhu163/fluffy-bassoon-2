Revision after the first live pair: in r1 controls 0–4 and measured state through control 5 matched, but the second five-action prediction diverged before any offset (first closure at 15 vs 169). A close at control 15 was also 0.54 m lateral to the handle. Do not attribute the r1 contrasts to the perturbation.

On each of the same eight **development** support/query pairs, generate one native closed-loop SFT command tape per scene, up to first success or H600. Replay that tape in each scored arm, even after the offset. The first approach within 10 cm of the target handle's collision-geometry center triggers one ±4 cm displacement of the OSC target in base Y; apply the wrong or inverse correction at this same update. The gate and geometry are evaluator-only. Capture student camera frames every five controls and around *all* public open-to-close transitions, independent of the hidden trigger. Log signed miss at the first post-approach closure when one occurs, and lagged handle displacement even when it does not. Keep scenes that never approach.

For every arm require exact native initial state/pixels and exact physics-state digest on every shared command through the intervention. For inverse arms require the whole neutral replay to reproduce the original native trajectory, terminal outcome and delivered length; both signs' neutral trajectories must also match. No comparison survives a failed gate. The 16 tape-generating rollouts plus 64 scored replays cost at most 48,000 controls and one GPU for 90 minutes. This is an open-loop mechanical positive control with a fixed nominal action budget; it cannot establish closed-loop adaptation or rank AD and DPT. A later learned test must qualify pre-intervention pairing and closed-loop response in the AD motor independently.
