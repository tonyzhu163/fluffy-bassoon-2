# Prior reward changes the rack retry, but a current camera sees the change

The frozen reward rule recovered four moving-rack placements and lost four stationary-rack placements on twelve new LIBERO-Goal bottle poses. It tied its fitted fixed baseline at **9/24 native placements**. A controller that waited five controls after observed lift and read the current camera scored **13/24** without a practice episode. Across-attempt feedback conveyed information about the persistent condition. The live frozen VLA reached **18/24** with current images. These controls show why a fixed replay by itself overstates the opportunity for ICL.

## What the prospective test measured

Each pose appeared under the released BASE and D02 rack configurations. D02 moved the receiver 7.5 cm near placement. At reset all twelve paired physics fingerprints and all twelve pooled agent-view images matched. The archived wrist-state prefixes through the lift trigger matched across regimes on all twelve poses. The menus contained nominal command replay, wrist tracking, and tracking shifted toward the published D02 displacement. The three motions use the same three successful fitting references, and every attempt costs 300 controls. The branch experiment replays verified nominal VLA openings, then executes each continuation in the native simulator. The support and query are different poses in the same regime. Under the prespecified cyclic pairing, four uninformative stationary misses redirected queries that nominal replay would have completed. In a secondary analysis that varies the distinct support pose, the history score ranged from 9 to 12 across the eleven cyclic offsets, against 9 for fixed replay. Selecting among the three actions with knowledge of the future native outcome would give 14/24. Those bounds apply to the three precommitted continuations. ## Controls and interpretation

The current-camera controller uses a class-centroid classifier of the change in agent-view RGB from reset, trained on development regime labels. It begins with nominal replay, then can switch to the shifted tracker. It selected the nominal action on all twelve stationary queries and the shifted action on ten of twelve moving queries. In the other two moving queries, receiver motion first occurred at control 146, after the camera decision. This control uses privileged labels at training time and ordinary RGB at evaluation. The live VLA comparator shares the first 24 controls, then replans every five controls from its own observations. Its actions can respond throughout placement. Of its six misses, only D02 pose 22 was recovered by any measured alternative. Even an evaluator with future outcomes choosing among the live VLA, all three continuations and the camera rule could reach only 19/24. [Camera decisions](current-camera.json) · [Live VLA verification](live-motor.json)

After seeing the single-reward tie, we replayed a **post hoc two-support test**. All policies were charged two practice attempts using the same unshifted tracker and a third query at a distinct pose. On development data the fitted rule chose shifted tracking if both rewards were zero and nominal replay otherwise. On the twelve prospective pose blocks it scored 11/24 on the third attempt, versus 9/24 using one reward and 9/24 ignoring both. Two rewards recovered two more stationary placements. Considering whether any of the three attempts succeeded, one and two rewards both achieved 14/24 versus 12/24 for the reward-blind policy. This comparison shares the original test branches and was specified after the first result. [Two-support replay](two-rewards.json)

**Paper decision.** Keep the moving rack as a test of the difference between inferring a persistent condition from an earlier trial and reacting to it in the current one. For the next fitting screen, begin with the live VLA and look for distinct recoverable errors before collecting a larger history panel. A strong candidate is a manipulation decision whose consequences begin before the condition becomes observable in the current episode. Keep the live reactive VLA, the current-image classifier, and the best fixed action, and score terminal placement alongside any-time native success.
